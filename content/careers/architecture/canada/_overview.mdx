## Why Canada for Architecture?

Canada combines quality architecture education with the most immigrant-friendly policies in the English-speaking world. While Canadian architecture schools may not have the global fame of the AA London or Cornell, they offer rigorous, professionally accredited programs with excellent career outcomes. The real advantage is what happens after graduation: Canada's Post-Graduation Work Permit (3 years) and Express Entry PR pathway make it the easiest country for your child to build a long-term career abroad.

**The financial picture:** A 4-year architecture degree in Canada costs ₹1.3-1.8 Crore — less than the US or UK. Living costs outside Toronto and Vancouver are very reasonable. And unlike many countries, Canada's work-while-studying rules are generous, allowing students to earn and gain experience simultaneously.

## The Path

1. **Class 11-12:** Focus on board exams (aim for 85%+). Prepare IELTS (target 7.0). Build a portfolio of creative work
2. **Summer before 12th:** Research programs — each university has different admission requirements and program structures. Some require portfolios, others don't
3. **October-February of 12th:** Apply directly to universities. Most application deadlines are January-February for September start
4. **February-May:** Conditional offers based on predicted/actual grades. Portfolio reviews where required
5. **After 12th results (June-July):** Meet conditions, accept offer, apply for Study Permit
6. **September:** Start university

## What Parents Should Know

- **Architecture education varies by school.** University of Toronto offers a 4-year Honours BA (then a 3.5-year M.Arch for professional qualification). University of Waterloo offers a unique co-op program where students alternate between study and paid work terms. UBC and McGill offer different structures. Research each carefully.
- **Professional licensure requires a Master's.** In Canada, to become a licensed architect, your child typically needs a Master of Architecture (M.Arch) from a CACB-accredited program after the undergraduate degree. The full path is: 4-year undergrad + 3-3.5 year M.Arch + Internship in Architecture Program (IAP) + licensure exams. Total time to licensure: 10+ years.
- **Portfolio requirements vary.** University of Waterloo requires a portfolio; University of Toronto does not require one for undergraduate admission. Check each school's requirements carefully.
- **Co-op at Waterloo is exceptional.** Waterloo's architecture program is the only co-op architecture program in Canada. Students complete 5 paid work terms at architecture firms, earning CAD 30,000-50,000 total over the degree while building professional experience. This is a major advantage.
- **PGWP + Express Entry = PR pathway.** After a 4-year degree, your child gets a 3-year open work permit. After gaining work experience, they can apply for PR through Express Entry. Architecture is on Canada's skilled occupations list.
- **Harsh winters.** Canadian winters are genuinely cold (Toronto: -15°C, Waterloo: -20°C in January). Budget for proper winter clothing and be prepared for short winter days.
