## Why TU Berlin for Architecture?

TU Berlin is one of Germany's leading technical universities, and its architecture department is among the most respected in Europe. Located in the heart of Berlin — Europe's most dynamic and architecturally exciting city — TU Berlin offers students a front-row seat to contemporary architecture in action. Berlin is a living laboratory of architectural history: Bauhaus modernism, post-war reconstruction, the Berlin Wall's urban scars, and a booming contemporary design scene coexist within the same city.

What makes TU Berlin extraordinary for Indian families: **zero tuition.** The semester fee of €312 includes a public transport pass for all of Berlin (worth €50/month alone). Your child's entire 3-year Bachelor costs approximately ₹30-35L including living expenses — less than a single year at a top US architecture school.

## How to Get In

TU Berlin has a selective admission process for architecture:

1. **German language proficiency:** TestDaF TDN 4 or DSH-2 (C1 level) is mandatory for the German-taught Bachelor program
2. **Academic qualifications:** Indian Class 12 with PCM. Direct admission is possible with strong marks (85%+); otherwise, Studienkolleg (1-year foundation) is required
3. **Mappe (Portfolio):** Submit a portfolio of 15-25 works demonstrating freehand drawing, 3D visualization, creative photography, model-making, and spatial observation. Quality matters more than polish — TU Berlin wants to see how you think, not just how you draw
4. **Apply through [uni-assist](https://www.uni-assist.de)** by July 15 for winter semester (October start)

**Portfolio tips:** Include freehand sketches of buildings and spaces, perspective drawings, still life compositions, photography that shows attention to light and form, and any model or sculpture work. German professors value process — include rough sketches and iterations, not just finished pieces.

**Timeline:** Start German in Class 10. Aim for B2 by end of Class 12, C1 by the time you arrive. Build portfolio throughout Class 11-12.

## Curriculum & Structure

3-year Bachelor of Science in Architecture (6 semesters), followed by a 2-year Master's:

- **Design Studios (Entwurf)** run every semester and are the core of the program. Projects increase in scale from rooms to buildings to urban districts
- **Construction and structural engineering** — German architecture education has a stronger technical focus than many other countries
- **History and theory of architecture** — extensive coverage of European, modernist, and contemporary architectural movements
- **Digital tools:** CAD, BIM (Revit), parametric design (Grasshopper/Rhino), and 3D visualization
- **Hands-on workshops:** TU Berlin has excellent fabrication facilities — wood, metal, concrete, and digital fabrication workshops
- **Thesis project** in the final semester of both Bachelor and Master programs
- **The Master's is essential for professional practice.** The Bachelor alone doesn't qualify for licensure in Germany. Plan for the full 5-year Bachelor + Master track

## Campus Life

TU Berlin's campus is in the Charlottenburg district, central Berlin:

- **Berlin is Europe's architecture capital.** Students are surrounded by works from every era — Schinkel's neoclassical buildings, Mies van der Rohe's Neue Nationalgalerie, Daniel Libeskind's Jewish Museum, David Chipperfield's James Simon Gallery. Weekend field trips are replaced by daily immersion
- **Architecture studios at TU Berlin** are collaborative, open spaces where students work alongside each other. The culture is less competitive and more supportive than at many Indian or US schools
- **Berlin is affordable for a European capital.** Rent for a shared flat (WG) is €400-600/month in most neighborhoods. Indian grocery stores are plentiful
- **Vibrant international community** — Berlin attracts students from all over the world. TU Berlin has a large international student body
- **Indian community in Berlin** is growing rapidly, with temples, cultural associations, and Indian restaurants in many neighborhoods
- **Part-time work is common** — many architecture students work in Berlin's many architecture offices (Architekturbüros) alongside their studies, gaining practical experience and income
