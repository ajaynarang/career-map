## Why the AA?

The Architectural Association is not a university — it is a private, independent school of architecture, and arguably the most influential architecture school in the world. Zaha Hadid, Rem Koolhaas, Richard Rogers, Peter Cook, and dozens of other architecture legends studied or taught here. The AA is where architectural ideas are born, debated, and pushed to their limits.

What makes the AA different from every other school: **radical experimentation is the norm, not the exception.** While most architecture schools teach students to design functional buildings within conventional parameters, the AA challenges students to question what architecture even is. Students explore digital fabrication, robotics, bio-architecture, responsive environments, and speculative design. If your child is a creative rebel who thinks differently — the AA is their home.

**Honest reality check for parents:** The AA is not for everyone. It is intensely theoretical and experimental. Students who want a conventional, practice-oriented education may find it frustrating. But students who embrace its culture emerge as some of the most creative and original thinkers in the profession.

## How to Get In

The AA does NOT use UCAS. Applications are made directly to the school:

1. **Online application** through the AA website. No specific deadline — the AA reviews applications on a rolling basis, but applying by January for September entry is recommended
2. **Portfolio submission:** This is the most important element. The AA wants to see creative work that demonstrates how you think — not just how you draw. Include experiments, explorations, observations, and projects that show genuine curiosity. Finished buildings or architectural drawings are NOT required or expected
3. **Interview:** All applicants are interviewed (in person or online). This is a conversation about your work, your ideas, and your motivation. The AA looks for passion, originality, and intellectual energy

**Key insight for Indian applicants:** The AA explicitly states that they do not look for prior architectural knowledge or technical drawing skills. They seek "creative, intelligent, and motivated individuals." A student who has spent time observing, questioning, and making things — even if they've never taken an architecture class — can get in. This is fundamentally different from NATA-based or JEE-based admissions.

**The AA Foundation Course:** If your child is unsure or needs a transition year, the AA offers a 1-year Foundation Course that introduces architectural thinking before committing to the full 5-year program.

## Curriculum & Structure

The AA Diploma is a 5-year integrated program (equivalent to RIBA Part 1 + Part 2):

- **First Year and Intermediate School (Years 1-3):** Foundation design skills, architectural history and theory, technical studies, and media studies. Students work in small "Units" led by practicing architects
- **Diploma School (Years 4-5):** Advanced design research in specialized units. Each unit has a distinct research agenda — topics range from robotic fabrication to urban ecology to virtual architecture. Students choose their unit based on interest
- **Unit system is the AA's signature.** Each unit operates like a small research lab with 10-12 students and a unit master (tutor). The unit's research theme shapes the year's design projects. Students present their work in "juries" — public critiques that are legendary for their intensity
- **Technical Studies** run alongside studio work — structures, environmental design, building technology, and professional practice
- **History and Theory Seminars** are intellectually demanding — students engage with philosophy, cultural theory, and architectural criticism
- **AA's workshops and labs** include digital fabrication (laser cutters, 3D printers, CNC), a hooke park woodland campus (for full-scale construction projects), and computational design labs

## Campus Life

The AA is housed in a Georgian townhouse at 36 Bedford Square, Bloomsbury — central London:

- **Bedford Square is intimate and intense.** The AA is small — about 600 students total across all programs. Everyone knows everyone. The building itself (a converted 18th-century townhouse) creates an atmosphere nothing like a conventional campus
- **The AA Bar** is famous — an informal social space where students, tutors, and visiting architects mix. Some of the most important architectural conversations in history have happened here
- **Lecture series:** The AA hosts weekly lectures by leading international architects, theorists, and thinkers. These are open to all students and are a significant part of the education
- **No campus amenities** like Indian or US universities — no gym, no sports facilities, no hostels. Students find their own accommodation in London (expensive) and create their social life through the school and the city
- **International community:** The AA is one of the most international schools in the world — students come from 60+ countries. Indian students are well-represented
- **AA Projects Review (end-of-year exhibition)** is a major event in the global architecture calendar. Critics, practitioners, and media from around the world attend
