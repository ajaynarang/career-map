## Why the USA for Architecture?

The United States is home to the world's most innovative architecture schools and the largest construction market globally. If your child wants to practice architecture at the cutting edge — computational design, sustainable buildings, parametric structures — American schools offer resources and research opportunities that few countries can match. Firms like SOM, Gensler, and Foster + Partners recruit directly from top US architecture programs.

**The investment is significant:** A 5-year B.Arch at a top US school costs ₹2.7-3.7 Crore. However, some schools offer substantial financial aid. Cooper Union in New York offers free tuition to all admitted students. Cornell, Rice, and others have need-based aid programs. Starting salaries for architects in the US are $55,000-75,000 (₹45-62 LPA), growing to $90,000-150,000+ (₹75 LPA-1.25 Crore) with experience and licensure.

## The Path

1. **Class 10-11:** Build a strong design portfolio — this is the most important part of your application. 15-20 pieces showing drawing, painting, photography, 3D models, and design thinking
2. **Class 11:** Take the SAT (aim for 1400+). Begin TOEFL/IELTS preparation
3. **Class 12 (August-January):** Apply via Common App or school-specific portals. Submit portfolio by deadline. Early Decision: November 1. Regular Decision: January 1-15
4. **March-April of 12th:** Admission decisions arrive. Compare financial aid packages
5. **Fall semester starts:** August-September. 5-year B.Arch programs begin immediately with design studios

## What Parents Should Know

- **Portfolio is king.** Unlike engineering admissions (which are test-driven), architecture admissions in the US revolve around the portfolio. A student with a 1350 SAT but a stunning portfolio will beat a student with a 1550 SAT and a mediocre portfolio. Invest time in portfolio development starting from Class 9-10.
- **B.Arch (5 years) vs. BA/BS in Architecture (4 years).** The 5-year B.Arch is a professional degree — graduates can proceed directly to licensure. A 4-year BA/BS in Architecture is a pre-professional degree that requires a 2-3 year Master of Architecture (M.Arch) afterward. If your child is certain about architecture, the 5-year B.Arch is more efficient.
- **Licensure in the US takes time.** After the degree, graduates need 3,740 hours of supervised work experience (AXP — Architecture Experience Program) and must pass the ARE (Architect Registration Examination, 6 divisions). Full licensure typically happens 3-5 years after graduation.
- **STEM OPT is not automatic.** Architecture is not classified as STEM at all schools. Some programs (MIT, for example) have STEM-designated architecture degrees that qualify for the 24-month STEM OPT extension (total 3 years of work authorization). Check each school's STEM designation carefully — it significantly affects your child's ability to work in the US after graduation.
- **Cooper Union is free.** Cooper Union in New York offers full-tuition scholarships to all admitted students. It's extremely selective (~5% acceptance for architecture), but the cost savings are enormous. Worth applying even as a stretch.
- **Design culture is different from India.** US architecture schools emphasize conceptual rigor, critical thinking, and experimentation. Students are expected to question conventions, not follow templates. This can be a culture shock for students from structured Indian coaching environments.
