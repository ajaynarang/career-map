## Why MIT Architecture?

MIT's architecture department is ranked #1 in the world, and for good reason. This is where architecture meets technology at the highest level. MIT's approach to architecture is fundamentally different from traditional schools: it treats buildings as complex systems to be designed, engineered, simulated, and optimized. Students learn computational design, digital fabrication, material science, and environmental engineering alongside architectural theory and studio work.

What makes MIT uniquely valuable for Indian families: **MIT is need-blind for international students.** If your child gets admitted, MIT guarantees to cover 100% of your family's demonstrated financial need. A family earning under $75,000/year (approximately ₹62 LPA) pays nothing. This means MIT can be cheaper than many Indian private colleges.

**Critical difference from other architecture schools:** MIT does NOT offer a B.Arch degree. It offers a BS in Architecture (Course 4), which is a 4-year pre-professional degree. To become a licensed architect, graduates must complete a Master of Architecture (M.Arch) afterward. Many MIT architecture graduates, however, go into computational design, tech, real estate development, or urban planning — fields where the M.Arch isn't required.

## How to Get In

MIT's overall acceptance rate is **3.9%**. Architecture is not a separate admission — students apply to MIT as a whole and declare architecture as their major during sophomore year.

**What this means practically:**
- Your child competes with all MIT applicants — the same pool applying for CS, physics, and engineering
- **No portfolio required** for undergraduate admission (unlike Cornell, Rice, or Cooper Union)
- Academic excellence is the baseline: near-perfect SAT/ACT scores, top grades, strong math and science
- **"Match" is what differentiates:** MIT wants builders, makers, and tinkerers. Olympiad medals, research projects, and creative builds matter enormously
- Students interested in architecture should demonstrate visual/spatial/creative interests in their application (art, design, photography, building models)

**Timeline:**
- Early Action: November 1 (non-binding, recommended)
- Regular Action: January 1
- Decisions: Mid-December (EA), mid-March (RA)

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year BS in Architecture (Course 4) — a pre-professional degree:

- **First year:** MIT's general institute requirements — calculus, physics, chemistry, biology, and humanities. No architecture courses yet
- **Sophomore year:** Declare architecture. Begin with design studios and architectural representation
- **Years 3-4:** Advanced studios, structures, environmental design, history/theory, and electives. Students can take courses across MIT — many combine architecture with computer science, mechanical engineering, or media arts
- **MIT Media Lab** — architecture students can work on projects at the legendary Media Lab, exploring the intersection of technology, design, and human experience
- **Computational Design** — MIT's architecture department is a global leader in algorithmic design, digital fabrication, and robotic construction. Students use tools like Grasshopper, Python, and machine learning in design
- **STEM designation:** MIT's BS in Architecture has a STEM CIP code, qualifying graduates for the 24-month STEM OPT extension (total 3 years of work authorization in the US)

## Campus Life

MIT sits along the Charles River in Cambridge, directly across from Boston:

- **Building 7 (the iconic dome)** and the surrounding architecture buildings house studios, workshops, and the Rotch Library (architecture library)
- **Fabrication labs** include CNC machines, laser cutters, industrial 3D printers, robotic arms, and a full materials testing lab. MIT's shops are among the best-equipped in the world
- **Cross-pollination is constant** — architecture students regularly collaborate with engineers, computer scientists, and business students on projects
- **Indian community is strong** — MIT has 200+ Indian international students with an active Indian Students Association
- **Boston/Cambridge is a college city** — 50+ universities in the area, vibrant culture, excellent Indian food scene
- **Weather:** Boston winters are harsh (November-March). Temperatures regularly drop below -10°C
