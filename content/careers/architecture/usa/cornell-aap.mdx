## Why Cornell AAP?

Cornell's College of Architecture, Art, and Planning (AAP) is one of America's top 3 architecture schools and the only Ivy League university offering an accredited 5-year B.Arch degree. This is a critical distinction: graduates leave with a professional degree and can proceed directly to licensure, while students at most other Ivy League schools receive a pre-professional BA and need an additional 2-3 year M.Arch to practice.

What makes Cornell AAP exceptional for Indian students: **it combines Ivy League prestige with a professional architecture degree.** Your child gets the Cornell brand, the Ivy League network, and a NAAB-accredited B.Arch — all in one package. Top global firms (SOM, KPF, Foster + Partners) actively recruit from Cornell AAP.

## How to Get In

Cornell AAP's B.Arch program has an acceptance rate of approximately **8-10%** — slightly more selective than Cornell's overall rate. The portfolio is the deciding factor.

**What Cornell wants to see in the portfolio:**
- 15-20 pieces of creative work
- **Freehand drawing is essential** — observational sketches, figure drawings, landscape studies
- 3D work — sculpture, models, installations
- Photography, painting, and digital work are welcome
- Evidence of **how you see and think about space** — not just technical skill
- Cornell explicitly states: "We do not expect a portfolio of architectural designs"

**Timeline:**
- Early Decision: November 1 (binding — if admitted, you must attend). Portfolio due by November 1
- Regular Decision: January 2. Portfolio due by January 2
- Decisions: Early December (ED), late March (RD)

**Strategy for Indian applicants:** Early Decision significantly boosts admission chances at Cornell (ED rate is ~20% vs. ~7% RD). If Cornell AAP is your child's first choice, applying ED is strongly recommended.

## Curriculum & Structure

5-year B.Arch, one of the most rigorous architecture programs in the US:

- **First year:** Foundation courses in design, drawing, and visual representation. Students work with physical models, hand drawing, and basic digital tools
- **Years 2-4:** Increasingly complex design studios — residential, commercial, institutional, and urban-scale projects. Structures, environmental systems, and history/theory courses run in parallel
- **Year 5:** Advanced studios with renowned visiting critics, and a comprehensive thesis project
- **Rome Program:** Cornell AAP operates a semester-long program in Rome — students live and study architecture in one of the world's richest architectural cities. Most B.Arch students attend in their 4th year
- **NYC Studio:** Upper-level students can spend a semester in Cornell's Manhattan studio, working on urban projects and connecting with NYC firms
- **Interdisciplinary access:** As a Cornell student, your child can take courses across the university — engineering, business, computer science, liberal arts

## Campus Life

Cornell sits on a stunning hilltop campus overlooking Cayuga Lake in Ithaca, New York:

- **AAP's Milstein Hall** (designed by Rem Koolhaas/OMA) is the architecture building — a striking modern structure that is itself a lesson in design. The building houses open studio spaces, a fabrication shop, and the architecture library
- **Fabrication facilities** include CNC routers, laser cutters, 3D printers, robotic arms, and a full wood/metal shop. Students build at every scale
- **Studio culture is intense** — architecture students at Cornell are known for their dedication. Late nights in Milstein Hall are common
- **Indian community is strong** — Cornell has 800+ Indian students and an active Indian Students Association
- **Ithaca is small but charming** — surrounded by gorges, waterfalls, and nature. Not a big city, but a focused academic environment
- **Weather warning:** Ithaca winters are harsh. Temperatures drop to -15°C regularly from December to March
