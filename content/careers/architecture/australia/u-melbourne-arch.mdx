## Why University of Melbourne for Architecture?

The University of Melbourne's Melbourne School of Design (MSD) is Australia's #1 architecture school and consistently ranks in the global top 20. Melbourne itself is Australia's design capital — a city that takes architecture seriously, with a culture of design appreciation that permeates everyday life (laneway culture, public art, adaptive reuse of heritage buildings, and innovative residential design).

What makes Melbourne attractive for Indian families: **no portfolio required for undergraduate admission** and a clear, structured pathway from Bachelor (3 years) to Master (2 years) to professional registration. The university admits based on academic merit, making it accessible to strong students who may not have had art/design training.

**The Melbourne Model:** The University of Melbourne follows the "Melbourne Model" — a broad undergraduate degree followed by a professional Master's. The Bachelor of Design (Architecture) provides a well-rounded education in design thinking, history, and technology. The Master of Architecture provides the professional qualification needed for registration as an architect.

## How to Get In

Apply directly through the University of Melbourne's online application portal:

1. **Apply online** at study.unimelb.edu.au — applications open year-round for February start (main intake). Recommended to apply by October-November for February entry
2. **Academic requirements:** Class 12 with 80%+ recommended. Strong math performance is advantageous
3. **IELTS:** 6.5 overall with minimum 6.0 in each component
4. **No portfolio required** for undergraduate admission — selection is based on academic merit
5. **Conditional offer** based on predicted/final results. Accept, pay deposit, and apply for student visa

**Strategic advantage:** Melbourne's pure academic merit-based admission means students who are strong in PCM but haven't built a formal art portfolio can still enter architecture. Design skills are developed from scratch within the program.

## Curriculum & Structure

3-year Bachelor of Design (Architecture), followed by a 2-year Master of Architecture:

- **Year 1:** Introduction to design thinking, architectural representation (drawing, modeling, digital tools), history of architecture, and construction technology. Students also take breadth subjects from other faculties (science, arts, commerce) as part of the Melbourne Model
- **Year 2:** Intermediate design studios with increasing complexity. Environmental design, structural systems, and digital design tools (Rhino, Revit, Grasshopper). Students begin developing their own design approach
- **Year 3:** Advanced design studios, research methods, and electives. Students can explore specializations like landscape, urbanism, or computational design
- **Melbourne School of Design building (MSD Building)** is an award-winning facility with open studios, fabrication workshops (laser cutters, 3D printers, CNC machines, robotic arm), a materials library, and exhibition spaces
- **The Master of Architecture (2 years)** is where professional skills are consolidated. It includes advanced studios, professional practice, thesis project, and is accredited for professional registration
- **Study abroad opportunities:** Exchange programs with architecture schools worldwide, including ETH Zurich, TU Delft, and Tsinghua University

## Campus Life

The University of Melbourne's Parkville campus is just north of Melbourne's CBD:

- **Melbourne is Australia's cultural capital.** The city has a thriving architecture and design scene — the Melbourne Design Week, Open House Melbourne, and numerous architecture exhibitions provide constant inspiration
- **MSD Building** is a hub of activity — studios are open 24/7, and the culture of late-night design work and collaboration is strong
- **Affordable compared to Sydney.** While Melbourne isn't cheap, it's more affordable than Sydney. Shared accommodation costs AUD 200-350/week in inner suburbs
- **Indian community is massive.** Melbourne has one of Australia's largest Indian populations, concentrated in suburbs like Clayton, Dandenong, and Footscray. Indian groceries, restaurants, and cultural events are abundant
- **Work opportunities:** Melbourne's architecture firms are accessible to students for part-time and internship work. The city's construction boom means opportunities are plentiful
- **Climate:** Melbourne has mild winters (rarely below 5°C) and warm summers. Known for "four seasons in a day" — changeable weather, but never extreme cold
- **Public transport** is efficient — trams, trains, and buses cover the metro area. Student discounts available
