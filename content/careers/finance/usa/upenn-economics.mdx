## Why UPenn Economics?

Here is the best-kept secret in undergraduate finance: you don't need to be in Wharton to get a Wharton-quality finance education at Penn. The College of Arts & Sciences economics department at UPenn is world-class in its own right — ranked top-10 nationally — and Penn College students have extensive access to Wharton courses, Wharton career services, and the same Wall Street recruiting pipeline.

**The Wharton crossover advantage:** UPenn is unique among universities in allowing College of Arts & Sciences students to take courses at Wharton. Your child can major in Economics at the College while taking corporate finance, investments, and accounting courses at Wharton. They graduate with a liberal arts degree enriched by Wharton's business curriculum — and they're recruited by the same banks and firms that recruit Wharton students.

**Why choose Penn Economics over Wharton directly?** Three reasons. First, it's slightly less competitive to get into the College (5.5%) versus Wharton (~5%). Second, the College gives your child a broader liberal arts education with more flexibility. Third, the economics department's PhD-track courses (mathematical economics, econometrics) provide rigorous theoretical training that Wharton's applied curriculum does not. For students aiming at quant finance, hedge funds, or PhD programs, Penn Economics can be the superior choice.

**Financial aid matches Wharton exactly.** Penn meets 100% of demonstrated need for all students, including internationals. Families earning under $75,000/year pay nothing.

## How to Get In

Apply to the College of Arts & Sciences at UPenn through the Common Application. The acceptance rate is approximately **5.5%**.

**What Penn looks for:**
- **Intellectual curiosity** — Penn wants students who love learning across disciplines, not just finance-focused strivers
- **Quantitative ability** — strong math grades and scores are essential for economics
- **"Why Penn" essay** — must demonstrate specific knowledge of Penn's programs, culture, and opportunities. Mention the Wharton crossover, specific professors, or research centers
- **Community engagement** — Penn values students who contribute to their communities and will contribute to Penn's campus life

**Timeline:**
- Early Decision: November 1 (binding — significantly higher acceptance rate, ~15-18%)
- Regular Decision: January 5
- Decisions: Late March

**Strategy for Indian applicants:** Apply Early Decision if Penn is the top choice. The ED advantage at Penn is substantial. In your essays, demonstrate awareness of the College-Wharton crossover opportunity — this shows you've done your research and understand Penn's unique structure.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year Bachelor of Arts in Economics. The College of Arts & Sciences provides a liberal arts framework with deep economics specialization.

- **BA in Economics:** Micro, Macro, Econometrics (statistics-heavy), and 5+ electives. Electives include financial economics, game theory, public economics, behavioral economics, and economic history
- **BA in Mathematical Economics:** The most rigorous track. Requires real analysis, probability theory, and advanced econometrics alongside economic theory. Ideal for quant finance or PhD preparation. Produces graduates who are aggressively recruited by hedge funds and quant trading firms
- **Wharton course access:** College students can take up to 25% of their courses at Wharton. This means your child can take Corporate Finance, Financial Accounting, Investments, and Derivatives — the same courses that Wharton students take
- **Dual degree opportunities:** The Huntsman Program (International Studies + Business) combines the College and Wharton. Individual students can also propose dual degrees
- **Liberal arts breadth:** College students take distribution requirements across humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, and quantitative reasoning. This breadth makes them stronger communicators and more versatile professionals
- **Research opportunities:** The Population Studies Center, Penn Institute for Economic Research (PIER), and individual faculty labs all welcome undergraduate researchers. Writing a senior thesis with a faculty advisor is common and strengthens PhD applications

## Finance Recruiting at Penn

The Wall Street recruiting infrastructure at Penn benefits ALL Penn students, not just Wharton:

- **Penn Career Services** — dedicated finance career advising for College students. Resume workshops, interview prep, and recruiting events
- **Wharton finance clubs** — many are open to College students. The Penn Undergraduate Finance Club draws members from all schools
- **On-campus recruiting:** Bulge bracket banks (Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley) and elite boutiques (Evercore, Lazard, Centerview) recruit from the College as well as Wharton
- **Junior summer internships** — the gateway to full-time offers. College economics students regularly place into IB, consulting, and asset management roles
- **Alumni network** — Penn's alumni in finance are extensive across Wharton and the College. The network is especially strong in New York, which is a 90-minute train ride from Philadelphia
- **Quant recruiting:** Jane Street, Citadel, Two Sigma, and D.E. Shaw actively recruit Penn Math Econ majors

## Campus Life

- **University City, Philadelphia** — Penn's campus is urban, walkable, and vibrant. The neighborhood has improved dramatically and offers excellent restaurants, parks, and cultural venues
- **Indian community** — Penn has a large Indian student body across all schools. Penn Masala (the famous a cappella group) is a cultural institution. The South Asian Society hosts Diwali, Holi, and cultural shows
- **Cross-school culture** — Penn's four undergraduate schools (College, Wharton, Engineering, Nursing) share a campus, dining halls, and social life. The cross-school interactions are a major advantage
- **Philadelphia is affordable** — compared to NYC, Boston, or LA, Philadelphia's cost of living is reasonable. Good Indian food in University City and in the broader Philadelphia area
- **NYC access** — 90 minutes by Amtrak. Many students travel to New York for recruiting events, networking, and weekend activities
- **Greek life** — significant at Penn. About 25-30% of students are in fraternities/sororities
- **Safety** — Penn's campus is well-secured with extensive safety services. University City is generally safe, though some surrounding areas require awareness
