## Why MIT for Finance?

MIT might seem like an unusual choice for a finance career — it's famous for engineering and science. But here's what most people don't know: MIT produces more quant traders, fintech founders, and quantitative finance professionals than almost any other university, including traditional business schools.

**The reason is simple: the highest-paying finance roles are math and technology roles.** Quant trading firms (Jane Street, Citadel, Two Sigma) don't hire people who studied traditional finance — they hire mathematicians, computer scientists, and engineers who can build trading algorithms, model risk, and process data at scale. MIT produces exactly these people.

**The compensation is extraordinary.** MIT graduates entering quant trading start at $200,000-400,000+ total compensation (₹1.7-3.3 Crore). Even MIT graduates entering traditional finance roles (Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley) command premium offers because of their quantitative skills. MIT's starting salaries in finance are the highest of any university globally.

**Need-blind for internationals.** MIT is one of the very few universities that is need-blind for all applicants, including international students. If your child gets in, MIT guarantees to meet 100% of financial need. Families earning under $75,000 pay nothing.

## How to Get In

MIT's admissions are the most competitive in the world:

1. **Apply at [apply.mit.edu](https://apply.mit.edu)** — MIT uses its own application system, not the Common App
2. **Early Action (November 1):** Non-binding, non-restrictive. Decisions in mid-December
3. **Regular Action (January 1):** Decisions in mid-March

**Reality check:** MIT receives 28,000+ applications for about 1,100 seats. The acceptance rate is 3.9%. For Indian applicants, the bar is exceptionally high.

**What MIT looks for in finance-oriented students:**
- **Mathematical ability at the highest level** — International Math Olympiad medals, AMC/AIME/USAMO performance, or equivalent
- **Building and making things** — MIT wants students who have created something: a trading bot, a fintech app, a data analysis project, a startup
- **Collaborative spirit** — MIT explicitly screens out "lone wolves." They want students who work well in teams
- **Genuine passion for solving hard problems** — not just good grades, but evidence of deep engagement with mathematical or computational challenges

**For PCM students:** If your child has competed in Math Olympiads (RMO/INMO/IMO) or has built coding projects, MIT is a strong fit. The ISI Admission Test preparation and MIT preparation overlap significantly in mathematical maturity.

## Curriculum & Structure

MIT offers several paths into quantitative finance:

- **Course 15-3 (Finance through MIT Sloan):** MIT's undergraduate finance degree — one of the very few STEM-designated business degrees in the US. Covers financial theory, derivatives pricing, risk management, and data-driven finance
- **Course 18C (Math with CS):** The most common path to quant trading. Combines pure mathematics with computer science — probability, stochastic calculus, algorithms, machine learning
- **Course 14-2 (Mathematical Economics):** Rigorous economics with heavy mathematical training — ideal for students wanting to work in economic research, policy, or academic finance
- **Course 18 (Mathematics):** Pure math — for students who want maximum mathematical depth before entering finance

Key features across all paths:
- **UROP (Undergraduate Research):** 85%+ of MIT students do paid research with faculty. Many finance-oriented students work at MIT's Laboratory for Financial Engineering
- **MIT Sloan Finance Group** — access to professors who are active researchers in derivatives, fintech, and market microstructure
- **Cross-registration with Harvard** — take courses at Harvard Business School or Harvard Economics
- **IAP (January term)** — short courses in trading, blockchain, quantitative methods, and other finance topics
- **STEM designation** for Course 15-3 means 3 years of OPT work authorization

## Campus Life

MIT sits along the Charles River in Cambridge, across from Boston:

- **MIT Trading Competition** — annual algorithmic trading competition that attracts quant firms as sponsors and recruiters
- **MIT Investment Management Club** — student-run investment fund managing real capital
- **MIT Fintech Conference** — student-organized conference bringing together fintech startups, VCs, and industry leaders
- **The culture is collaborative** — MIT has a no-grade-disclosure policy for first semester (pass/no-record), reducing competition and encouraging teamwork
- **Boston is a finance hub** — Fidelity Investments, State Street, and numerous hedge funds are headquartered in Boston
- **Indian community is strong** — 200+ Indian international students, active ISA and Sangam organisations
- **Problem sets (P-sets) define the experience** — MIT coursework is famously demanding. Finance students at MIT learn to handle pressure and solve problems under time constraints — skills that translate directly to trading floors
