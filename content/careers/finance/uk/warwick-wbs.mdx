## Why Warwick for Finance?

Warwick Business School is the UK's best-kept secret in finance education. While LSE, Oxford, and Cambridge dominate the headlines, Warwick consistently places graduates into the same bulge bracket banks and top consulting firms — often at higher rates than better-known competitors. Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, and Morgan Stanley all list Warwick as a "target school" for UK graduate recruitment, alongside Oxford, Cambridge, LSE, and Imperial.

**The MORSE programme is Warwick's secret weapon.** BSc MORSE (Mathematics, Operational Research, Statistics & Economics) is one of the most quantitative undergraduate degrees in the UK. It combines rigorous mathematics with applied economics, statistics, and operational research — producing graduates who are aggressively sought by quant trading firms, hedge funds, and data-driven finance roles. MORSE graduates regularly receive offers from Optiver, Jane Street, Citadel, and G-Research alongside the traditional banks.

**BSc Accounting and Finance** is ranked #1 in the UK by The Guardian. The programme provides exemptions from several professional accounting exams (ACCA, ICAEW), giving your child a head start in qualifying as a chartered accountant.

**The value proposition is compelling.** Warwick's international tuition (£30,000-34,000/year) is significantly lower than LSE (£30,384) when you factor in London's much higher living costs. Living in Coventry/Warwick costs roughly £8,000-10,000/year versus £14,000-18,000 in London. Over three years, Warwick can save your family £15,000-20,000 compared to LSE — with equivalent career outcomes.

## How to Get In

1. **Apply through UCAS** at [ucas.com](https://www.ucas.com) — **January 31 deadline**
2. **Admissions tests:** TMUA (Test of Mathematics for University Admissions) required for MORSE and some Economics programmes. MAT accepted as alternative. Strong TMUA performance significantly strengthens your application
3. **UCAS Personal Statement:** Must demonstrate genuine engagement with your chosen subject — economics reading, quantitative projects, financial literacy work
4. **Conditional Offer:** A*A*A for MORSE (with A* in Maths); A*AA for Economics and Accounting & Finance
5. **No interview** — Warwick does not interview undergraduate applicants

**What Warwick looks for:**
- **Quantitative excellence** — TMUA performance is critical for MORSE and Economics. Strong A-Level Mathematics (or equivalent) is non-negotiable
- **Subject passion** — evidence of reading economics books, following financial news, or exploring quantitative problems beyond the curriculum
- **Academic consistency** — strong predicted grades across all subjects, not just maths
- **Independence** — Warwick values students who take initiative in their learning

**Comparison with LSE for Indian applicants:** Warwick's acceptance rate (10-15%) is higher than LSE's BSc Economics (6-8%), making it a more realistic option for strong Indian students. The TMUA is required at both, so preparation serves both applications. Many Indian students apply to both Warwick and LSE through UCAS.

## Curriculum & Structure

3-year BSc (some programmes offer 4-year variants with a year abroad or year in industry).

### BSc MORSE (the flagship)
- **Year 1:** Linear algebra, analysis, probability, statistics, microeconomics, macroeconomics, and programming (R/Python)
- **Year 2:** Operational research, mathematical statistics, econometrics, optimization, and electives
- **Year 3:** Advanced electives in financial mathematics, stochastic processes, game theory, Bayesian statistics, and a final-year project
- **Why MORSE is special:** The combination of rigorous mathematics with economics and statistics produces graduates who can work in any quantitative role. It's the closest UK equivalent to MIT's Course 18C (Mathematics with Computer Science) for finance applications

### BSc Accounting and Finance
- **Year 1:** Financial accounting, management accounting, microeconomics, quantitative methods, and law
- **Year 2:** Corporate finance, financial reporting, taxation, audit, and data analytics
- **Year 3:** Advanced corporate finance, derivatives, international accounting standards, and a research project
- **Professional exemptions:** Graduates receive exemptions from 6-9 ACCA exam papers and multiple ICAEW modules, saving 1-2 years of professional qualification time

### BSc Economics
- **Year 1:** Micro, Macro, mathematical economics, and statistics
- **Year 2:** Econometrics, advanced micro/macro, and electives (development economics, monetary economics, game theory)
- **Year 3:** Dissertation + advanced electives. Strong options in financial economics and behavioural economics

### Optional: Year in Industry / Year Abroad
- Many programmes offer 4-year variants with a paid placement year at a bank, consulting firm, or tech company (Year in Industry) or a year at a partner university (Year Abroad). The Year in Industry is especially valuable — students earn £25,000-40,000 during their placement year and return to final year with real-world experience and often a return offer

## Finance Recruiting

- **Target school status:** Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley, Barclays, and HSBC all recruit directly from Warwick. The university hosts on-campus presentations, networking events, and first-round interviews
- **Warwick Finance Societies** — the Economics Society, Investment Club, and Warwick Finance Society organize speaker events with City professionals, stock pitch competitions, and networking trips to London
- **Spring weeks** — first-year students apply for 1-week paid programmes at investment banks. Warwick students are strongly represented
- **Summer internships** — the gateway to graduate offers. Warwick places well into IB, consulting, accounting, and quant roles
- **Warwick alumni in the City** — strong and supportive network. Many managing directors and partners at major firms are Warwick graduates
- **London is 1 hour by train** — Coventry/Warwick to London Euston in 60-70 minutes. Students regularly commute for recruiting events

## Campus Life

- **Campus university** — unlike LSE or UCL, Warwick has a self-contained campus with its own arts centre (one of the largest in the UK), sports facilities, lake, and student union. This creates a strong community feeling
- **The Arts Centre** — Warwick Arts Centre hosts theatre, music, cinema, and exhibitions. It's the largest arts venue in the UK outside London
- **Indian community is substantial** — Warwick has a large Indian and South Asian student body. The Indian Society, Hindu Society, and Sikh Society are active. Diwali celebrations are among the biggest events on campus. Coventry has a significant Indian population with excellent restaurants
- **Accommodation** — Warwick guarantees first-year accommodation on campus. Rooms range from £130-200/week
- **Cost of living** — Coventry and the Warwick area are affordable by UK standards. £8,000-10,000/year for living expenses (versus £14,000-18,000 in London)
- **Social life** — the Warwick Students' Union has 300+ clubs and societies. Pop!, Circling, and other social events are part of Warwick culture
- **Weather:** Midlands weather is mild but grey. Expect rain and overcast skies October-March. Temperatures rarely drop below -2°C
