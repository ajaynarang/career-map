## Why the UK for Finance?

London is home to the Bank of England, Lloyd's of London, HSBC, Barclays, and the European headquarters of every major US bank. The City of London and Canary Wharf together employ over 400,000 people in financial services. If your child studies finance or economics at LSE, Oxford, or Cambridge, they are studying in the heart of this ecosystem.

**The 3-year degree advantage.** While US degrees take 4 years, UK economics and finance degrees are 3 years — and deeply specialized from day one. Your child starts studying economics immediately, not after 2 years of general education. This means entering the workforce a full year earlier, saving a year of tuition, and starting to earn sooner.

**The financial picture:** A 3-year UK degree costs ₹1.3-2 Crore total — significantly less than a 4-year US degree (₹2.3-3 Crore). The 2-year Graduate Route visa after graduation provides a clear pathway to gain work experience in one of the world's top financial centres.

## The Path

1. **Class 11-12:** Excel in Math (this is the most important subject for UK economics admissions). Prepare for IELTS (7.0+)
2. **Summer before 12th:** Research programs through UCAS. Attend virtual open days. Read economics books (Ha-Joon Chang, Tim Harford) for personal statement material
3. **September-October of 12th:** Apply through UCAS. **October 15 deadline for Oxford and Cambridge.** January 31 deadline for LSE and all others. Maximum 5 choices
4. **November-January:** Interviews for Oxford (if shortlisted). LSE and Cambridge may require TMUA scores
5. **After 12th results (June-July):** Meet conditional offer requirements. Apply for Student visa
6. **September:** Start university

## What Parents Should Know

- **UCAS limits you to 5 choices** — and you cannot apply to both Oxford AND Cambridge. Choose strategically. A common combination: Oxford OR Cambridge + LSE + Warwick + UCL + one safety.
- **Academic focus over extracurriculars.** Unlike the US, UK universities primarily care about academic ability and genuine intellectual interest in economics/finance. The UCAS Personal Statement should demonstrate deep reading and analytical thinking about economics — not generic leadership stories.
- **TMUA (Test of Mathematics for University Admissions)** is required or recommended by LSE, Cambridge, and Warwick for economics. It tests mathematical reasoning at a level beyond standard boards. Prepare specifically for this.
- **London is expensive.** Living costs in London are ₹12-18 lakh per year. LSE students should budget for this. Oxford and Cambridge are significantly cheaper for living costs.
- **The Graduate Route visa is straightforward.** After completing a UK degree, your child gets an automatic 2-year work visa with no employer sponsorship needed. This is simpler than the US H-1B lottery.
- **Internship spring weeks.** UK banks offer "spring weeks" (1-week paid internships) in the first year of university. These are the gateway to summer internships and full-time offers. Encourage your child to apply from day one.
- **ACA/ACCA qualifications.** The UK equivalent of CA is ACA (ICAEW) or ACCA. Many economics graduates pursue these alongside their first job. Employers often sponsor and pay for the qualification.
