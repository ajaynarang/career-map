## Why LSE?

LSE is the world's leading university for social sciences — and that's not marketing, it's fact. More heads of state, central bank governors, and Nobel laureates in economics have studied at LSE than at any other university. Located in the heart of London, steps from the City of London and Canary Wharf, LSE is the single best place in Europe to study economics and finance.

**The City of London connection is unmatched.** LSE's location in central London means your child is 15 minutes from Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, and Barclays. Spring weeks, summer internships, and networking events are all within walking distance. LSE sends more graduates into the City of London's financial sector than any other university — including Oxford and Cambridge.

**For Indian students, LSE is especially relevant.** LSE has deep historical ties to India — from BR Ambedkar to Amartya Sen to Raghuram Rajan (former RBI Governor). The Indian community at LSE is one of the largest of any international cohort. And the 3-year degree means your child enters the workforce a full year earlier than US counterparts, saving a year of tuition.

## How to Get In

1. **Apply through UCAS** at [ucas.com](https://www.ucas.com) — **January 31 deadline.** You can apply to up to 5 universities through UCAS, and LSE can be one of them
2. **TMUA (Test of Mathematics for University Admissions):** Required for BSc Economics, BSc Finance, and most quantitative programmes. The TMUA is taken in October-November of 12th grade. It tests mathematical reasoning at a higher level than standard boards
3. **UCAS Personal Statement:** One statement for all 5 UCAS choices. For LSE, it must demonstrate genuine intellectual engagement with economics — books read, articles analysed, economic concepts discussed
4. **Conditional Offer:** LSE makes offers conditional on 12th board results (typically 95%+ overall with 95%+ in Mathematics)

**Reality check:** LSE's BSc Economics is one of the most competitive undergraduate programmes in the world. It receives about 2,500 applications for 200 seats. The effective acceptance rate for BSc Economics is about 6-8%.

**What LSE looks for:**
- **Academic excellence in mathematics** — this is non-negotiable. TMUA performance is critical
- **Intellectual engagement with economics** — your child must demonstrate that they read, think, and argue about economic ideas. Mention specific economists, theories, or current economic debates in the personal statement
- **No interview** — LSE does not interview candidates. The personal statement and TMUA score carry enormous weight
- **Super-curriculars over extra-curriculars** — LSE cares less about sports captaincy and more about economics essay competitions, reading groups, or self-directed research

## Curriculum & Structure

3-year BSc (some programmes offer 4-year variants with a study abroad year):

- **BSc Economics:** Micro, Macro, Econometrics, and a wide range of electives from Development Economics to Behavioural Economics to Financial Economics. Deeply quantitative — you need strong calculus and statistics
- **BSc Finance:** Unique in the UK — one of the very few undergraduate Finance degrees at a top university. Covers corporate finance, investments, derivatives, and financial modelling
- **BSc Actuarial Science:** The best actuarial programme in the UK. Provides exemptions from many IFoA (Institute and Faculty of Actuaries) exams
- **BSc Mathematics & Economics / BSc Econometrics & Mathematical Economics:** The most quantitative options — ideal for quant finance careers
- **LSE Summer School** — many students take additional courses during summer, accelerating their learning
- **General Course students** from US universities spend a year at LSE, enriching the classroom with diverse perspectives

## Campus Life

LSE's campus is compact — a cluster of buildings in central London near the Aldwych:

- **The campus is urban** — there's no sprawling green campus like at Oxford or Cambridge. But London itself is the campus. Museums, theatres, restaurants, and the financial district are all within walking distance
- **LSE Finance Society and Economics Society** — among the most active student societies in the UK. Regular speaker events with hedge fund managers, central bankers, and policy makers
- **Spring Weeks** — UK banks offer 1-week paid programmes in the Easter break of first year. These are the gateway to summer internships. LSE students dominate spring week applications
- **The LSE Students' Union** is one of the most politically active in the UK — debates on economic policy, globalisation, and inequality are constant
- **Indian community** — the LSE India Society is one of the largest national societies at LSE. Events, networking, and cultural celebrations throughout the year
- **Living in London is expensive** — budget £12,000-18,000/year for accommodation and living costs. LSE halls of residence are available but limited
