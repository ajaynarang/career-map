## Why Cambridge for Finance?

Cambridge's Economics Tripos is one of the most rigorous and mathematically demanding economics programmes in the world. While Oxford's approach is broader (PPE includes philosophy and politics), Cambridge goes deep into pure economics — microeconomic theory, macroeconomic dynamics, econometrics, and mathematical economics at a level that few other undergraduate programmes match.

**This mathematical depth is exactly what the finance industry values most.** Cambridge Economics graduates are heavily recruited by quantitative hedge funds (Citadel, Marshall Wace, Man Group), investment banks, and consulting firms. The reason is straightforward: Cambridge economics training develops the kind of rigorous analytical thinking that separates great analysts from average ones.

**Historical significance:** John Maynard Keynes — the most influential economist of the 20th century — studied and taught at Cambridge. The Cambridge tradition in economics is one of original thinking and challenging orthodoxy. Alfred Marshall, Joan Robinson, Piero Sraffa, and Amartya Sen all called Cambridge home.

**For PCM students:** Cambridge Economics requires A*A*A with a near-perfect score in Mathematics. The TMUA exam is heavily mathematical. PCM students are ideally prepared — your child's JEE-level math preparation translates directly to TMUA success.

## How to Get In

1. **Apply through UCAS** at [ucas.com](https://www.ucas.com) — **October 15 deadline** (same as Oxford; remember, you cannot apply to both Oxford AND Cambridge)
2. **TMUA (Test of Mathematics for University Admissions):** Required. Taken in October-November. Tests mathematical thinking and reasoning — not rote calculation. Two papers: mathematical thinking, and mathematical reasoning in context
3. **Shortlisting:** Based on application, predicted grades, personal statement, and TMUA score. About 60-70% of applicants are shortlisted for interview (Cambridge shortlists more generously than Oxford)
4. **Interview (December):** Typically 2 interviews. You'll be given a problem — perhaps a graph to interpret, a mathematical question, or an economic scenario — and asked to think through it. Interviewers assess how you respond to new information and hints
5. **Conditional Offer (January):** Offers are conditional on board results and sometimes on achieving a specific STEP/TMUA score

**Reality check:** Cambridge Economics receives about 1,300 applications for approximately 155 seats. The acceptance rate is about 12% — slightly higher than Oxford E&M but don't be misled; the applicant pool is exceptionally strong.

**Cambridge vs. Oxford for Economics:**
- Cambridge is more mathematically rigorous — if your child loves pure mathematics and wants the deepest analytical training, choose Cambridge
- Oxford E&M is broader — combining economics with management. If your child wants a more business-oriented education, choose Oxford
- Both produce exceptional finance professionals, but through different paths

## Curriculum & Structure

3-year BA Economics (the "Economics Tripos"):

- **Part I (Year 1):** Microeconomics, Macroeconomics, Quantitative Methods (statistics and mathematics for economists), and either Politics or a paper from another Tripos (History, Math, etc.)
- **Part IIA (Year 2):** Microeconomics, Macroeconomics, Econometrics, plus one elective from options like International Economics, Labour Economics, or British Economic History
- **Part IIB (Year 3):** Highly specialised — choose from Advanced Microeconomics, Advanced Macroeconomics, Finance & Financial Economics, Development Economics, Behavioural Economics, Game Theory, Industrial Organisation. Year 3 also includes a dissertation — original research on a topic of your choice
- **Supervision system** — Cambridge's version of Oxford's tutorial. Weekly small-group sessions (2-3 students) with a supervisor. You submit work, receive detailed feedback, and debate ideas
- **Mathematical rigor increases each year** — by Year 3, you're doing constrained optimisation, dynamic programming, and stochastic calculus in your economics courses

## Campus Life

Cambridge is a medieval university city — compact, beautiful, and intellectually intense:

- **College system** — like Oxford, every student belongs to a college (Trinity, King's, St John's, etc.). Your college is your home: accommodation, meals, social life, and supervisions all happen there
- **Trinity College** has produced more Nobel Prize winners than most countries. King's College Chapel is one of the most beautiful buildings in England
- **Cambridge University Economics Society** — runs speaker events, trading competitions, and career workshops
- **Cambridge Finance and Investment Society** — connects students with City of London professionals
- **The Marshall Society** — Cambridge's elite economics discussion society, named after Alfred Marshall. Regular events with prominent economists and policy makers
- **Punting on the Cam** — a quintessentially Cambridge experience. Students relax by punting (flat-bottomed boats) along the River Cam past the historic college backs
- **Cambridge is quieter than London** — this is a feature, not a bug. The environment is designed for deep study and intellectual development. London is just 50 minutes away by train for networking and interviews
- **Indian community** — Cambridge India Society and active networks. Several colleges have specific outreach for Indian students
