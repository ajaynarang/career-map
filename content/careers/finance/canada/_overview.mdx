## Why Canada for Finance?

Canada offers something no other country can match: a top-tier finance education combined with a near-guaranteed pathway to Permanent Residency. Toronto's Bay Street is home to the Big Five Canadian banks — together managing over CAD $6 trillion in assets. Canada's pension funds (CPP Investments, Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan) are among the world's most sophisticated institutional investors. And the growing fintech sector in Toronto and Vancouver creates new opportunities every year.

**The immigration advantage is unbeatable.** After a 4-year degree, your child gets a 3-year open work permit (PGWP). After 1 year of skilled work in finance, they can apply for Permanent Residency through Express Entry. No lottery, no employer sponsorship for PR. Finance roles qualify as skilled work under NOC codes. This clarity is why Indian families increasingly prefer Canada over the US.

**The financial picture:** A 4-year commerce/finance degree costs ₹1.5-2 Crore — cheaper than the US or UK. The ability to work 20 hours per week during studies and full-time during breaks helps offset costs. Canadian minimum wage is CAD $16-17/hour.

## The Path

1. **Class 11-12:** Focus on board exams (85%+ needed for top programs). Prepare for IELTS (aim for 7.0+)
2. **Summer before 12th:** Research universities. Each Canadian university has its own application portal
3. **October-February of 12th:** Apply directly to universities. Rotman Commerce (U of Toronto) deadline: January. Ivey AEO (Western) deadline: February
4. **February-May:** Conditional offers arrive based on predicted/actual grades. Some programs require supplementary applications (video essays, short answers)
5. **After 12th results (June-July):** Meet conditions, accept offer, apply for Study Permit
6. **September:** Start university

## What Parents Should Know

- **No SAT required for most programs.** Canadian universities admit primarily on 12th board results (85-93%+ for top business programs). Some programs ask for supplementary essays or video interviews.
- **Rotman vs. Ivey — different models.** Rotman Commerce (U of Toronto) admits directly after Class 12 into a 4-year BComm. Ivey (Western) has AEO (Advanced Entry Opportunity) — conditional admission after Class 12, but students enter Ivey's HBA program in 3rd year after completing 2 years of any undergraduate degree at Western. Both models work well.
- **Co-op and internships are integrated.** Many Canadian business programs include mandatory co-op terms where students earn CAD 20,000-30,000 per term while gaining real work experience at banks and consulting firms.
- **CPA (Chartered Professional Accountant)** is Canada's version of CA. Commerce graduates can pursue CPA alongside their first job. Employers typically sponsor the CPA qualification.
- **Bay Street recruitment follows a structured timeline.** Similar to Wall Street, Canadian banks recruit through summer internship programs. Networking starts in first year.
- **PR pathway is clear.** Finance roles (financial analysts, accountants, auditors) are eligible for Express Entry. After 1 year of Canadian work experience, your child can apply for PR. Processing time is typically 6-8 months.
- **Toronto is expensive but manageable.** Living costs in Toronto are high by Canadian standards but still lower than New York or London. Western University (London, Ontario) is significantly cheaper for living costs.
