## Why St. Stephen's?

St. Stephen's College is India's most storied educational institution — 143 years of producing the country's intellectual elite. Its economics programme is legendary: alumni include former RBI Governors, Finance Secretaries, World Bank economists, and the founders of some of India's largest financial institutions.

**What makes St. Stephen's different from SRCC:** While SRCC is the #1 college for commerce and direct industry placements, St. Stephen's excels at producing thinkers, policy makers, and leaders who shape systems. If your child wants to work at McKinsey, join the IAS, pursue a PhD at Cambridge, or eventually lead economic policy in India, St. Stephen's is the place.

**The economics programme is deeply analytical.** Microeconomics, macroeconomics, econometrics, mathematical economics, and game theory — taught by some of India's finest economists. PCM students thrive here because the programme demands strong mathematical reasoning.

## How to Get In

St. Stephen's has a unique admission process:

1. **CUET Score (85% weightage):** Take the CUET exam in May. Choose Mathematics, Economics, and English as your subjects
2. **St. Stephen's Interview (15% weightage):** Shortlisted candidates are called for an interview at the college (June-July)
3. **Combined Merit List:** CUET score (85%) + Interview score (15%) determines admission

**The interview is what sets St. Stephen's apart.** It's not a stress interview — it's a conversation. The panel (typically 3-4 faculty members) wants to understand your intellectual curiosity. They may ask about current economic events, your reading habits, a math problem, or why you want to study economics.

**Reality check:** St. Stephen's BA Economics (Hons) has roughly 40-50 seats. With CUET being taken by 15 lakh+ students, you need a near-perfect CUET percentile AND a strong interview to get in. It's arguably the hardest undergraduate admission in India.

**Preparation tip:** For the interview, read The Hindu editorial page daily, follow economic debates (budget, monetary policy, inflation), and be ready to discuss at least 2-3 books you've read on economics or related topics. "Freakonomics" (Levitt & Dubner) and "The Argumentative Indian" (Amartya Sen) are good starting points.

## Curriculum & Structure

3-year BA Economics (Honours) under the University of Delhi framework:

- **Year 1:** Introductory Microeconomics, Introductory Macroeconomics, Mathematical Methods, Statistical Methods
- **Year 2:** Intermediate Micro/Macro, Econometrics, Public Finance, Money & Banking
- **Year 3:** International Economics, Development Economics, Game Theory, electives in Financial Economics and Indian Economy
- **Dissertation** in the final year — original research on an economic topic
- **College tutorials** — small-group teaching (4-6 students per tutorial) supplementing university lectures. This Oxford-style tutorial system is unique to St. Stephen's

## Campus Life

Located in the heart of North Campus, University of Delhi:

- **The Residence (hostel)** — limited seats, but hostel life at St. Stephen's is a defining experience. Midnight debates, intellectual discussions, and a culture of questioning everything
- **Literary and Debating Society** — one of India's oldest, producing champion debaters who go on to become lawyers, journalists, and politicians
- **The Stephanian** — the college magazine has published essays by students who later became ministers, diplomats, and Nobel Prize-nominated economists
- **Cross-pollination** — economics students interact with students from English, History, Philosophy, and Physics, creating a truly liberal arts environment
- **Annual festivals** — Harmony (cultural fest) and various department festivals bring the campus alive

## After St. Stephen's

The typical St. Stephen's Economics graduate has three main paths:

1. **Consulting/Banking:** McKinsey, BCG, Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan — ₹15-25 LPA starting
2. **Higher Studies:** MA Economics at DSE (Delhi School of Economics), then PhD at Ivy League universities. St. Stephen's sends more students to top global PhD programmes than any other Indian college
3. **Civil Services/Policy:** IAS, IES (Indian Economic Service), RBI — many of India's top bureaucrats and economic policy makers are Stephanians
4. **MBA:** Top performers go on to IIM-A/B/C or international business schools (Wharton, LBS, INSEAD)
