## Why Defence?

Defence is unlike any other career on this list. It's not about salaries or placements — it's about serving the nation, leading soldiers, and being part of something larger than yourself. That said, the practical benefits are exceptional: free training, a salary from day one, free housing and healthcare for life, subsidized canteen facilities, and a pension after 20 years. Very few civilian careers offer this combination of purpose and security.

**The numbers:** Officers start at ₹56,100/month (Level 10, 7th Pay Commission) plus Dearness Allowance, Military Service Pay (₹15,500/month), and allowances. Effective CTC at entry level: ₹10-12 LPA. By Captain/Major rank (age 28-32): ₹15-20 LPA effective. After 20 years, officers retire with a pension of ₹40,000-80,000/month for life, plus medical benefits. Post-retirement, many officers join the corporate sector at senior levels, earning ₹25-50+ LPA.

## The Path

### Route 1: NDA (National Defence Academy) — after Class 12
1. **Class 12 with PCM:** Score 60%+ aggregate (minimum requirement). Higher marks keep civilian backup options open
2. **UPSC NDA Written Exam:** Conducted twice a year (April and September). Paper 1: Mathematics (300 marks), Paper 2: General Ability (600 marks)
3. **SSB Interview:** 5-day assessment at a Services Selection Board center. Tests Officer-Like Qualities (OLQs) — leadership, initiative, teamwork, and psychological stability
4. **Medical Examination:** Rigorous physical and medical screening
5. **Merit List:** Final selection based on written exam + SSB marks
6. **NDA Training (3 years):** At NDA Khadakwasla, Pune — combined training for Army, Navy, and Air Force cadets
7. **Service Academy Training (1 year):** After NDA — IMA Dehradun (Army), Naval Academy Ezhimala (Navy), or AFA Dundigal (Air Force)
8. **Commissioning:** Receive your rank of Lieutenant/Sub Lieutenant/Flying Officer. Begin active service

### Route 2: CDS (Combined Defence Services) — after graduation
1. Complete a Bachelor's degree (any discipline for Army; Engineering for technical branches; Physics/Math for Air Force)
2. CDS written exam (UPSC, twice a year) + SSB interview
3. Training at IMA/OTA/Naval Academy/AFA — duration varies by branch

### Route 3: NCC Special Entry / Technical Entry
- NCC 'C' certificate holders: Direct SSB entry (no written exam)
- 10+2 Technical Entry Scheme (TES): For PCM students with 70%+ — direct entry to engineering + military training

## What Parents Should Know

- **Physical fitness is a filter, not a formality.** Every year, thousands of students clear the NDA written exam but fail the physical test or medical examination. Start fitness training in Class 9 — running, push-ups, pull-ups, and swimming. Common medical disqualifiers: poor eyesight (uncorrectable), flat feet, color blindness, and being underweight/overweight. Get a medical check-up early to identify any issues.
- **SSB is where 80-90% of candidates are eliminated.** The SSB interview is a 5-day psychological and physical assessment. It tests genuine personality, not memorized answers. SSB assessors are trained psychologists who evaluate Officer-Like Qualities: initiative, courage, determination, social adaptability, cooperation, and sense of responsibility. There is no shortcut — genuine personality development over years is the only preparation that works.
- **NCC is the cheat code.** If your child's school offers NCC (National Cadet Corps), enroll in Class 9. An NCC 'C' certificate provides: (a) direct entry to SSB without the written exam through the NCC Special Entry Scheme, (b) bonus marks in NDA/CDS, and (c) demonstrated commitment to defence during SSB.
- **The commitment is real.** Minimum service obligation after NDA: 10 years (Army), 14 years (Navy/Air Force). This is not a "try it and see" career. Make sure your child genuinely wants to serve — SSB assessors can identify reluctant candidates instantly.
- **Women in defence.** Women can now join the armed forces through CDS, AFCAT, SSC, and — since 2022 — NDA. Permanent commission is available for women in all branches. This is a rapidly expanding opportunity.
- **Have a civilian backup.** Defence selection rates are very low (overall 1-2%). Smart families prepare for NDA while simultaneously keeping JEE/NEET/other exam options open. Many successful officers actually had alternative options but chose defence out of genuine calling.
- **Post-retirement career is excellent.** Officers retire between ages 40-54 (depending on rank). They enter the corporate sector with leadership, discipline, and management skills that companies value highly. Defence officers are sought after in security, logistics, operations, project management, and consulting. Many join at VP/Director level.
