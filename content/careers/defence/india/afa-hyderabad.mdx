## Why the Air Force Academy?

The Air Force Academy (AFA) at Dundigal, Hyderabad is where India's Air Force officers are trained. If your child dreams of flying fighter jets — Sukhoi Su-30MKI, Tejas LCA, Rafale, MiG-29 — this is where that dream begins. The AFA is the only training institution for IAF officers, and its Flying Branch produces some of the most skilled military pilots in the world.

What makes the Air Force unique among the three services: **it's the most technologically advanced branch.** IAF officers work with cutting-edge fighter aircraft, radar systems, missile batteries, satellite communications, and cyber warfare systems. For PCM students who love physics and technology, the Air Force offers a career that combines technical mastery with the thrill of flight.

**Critical note for parents:** The Flying Branch (pilot) is the most competitive and demanding entry in all of Indian defence services. Vision requirements are strict, the Computerised Pilot Selection System (CPSS) has a high failure rate, and the medical standards are the toughest. However, the Air Force also has Technical Branch (for engineers) and Ground Duty Branch (administration, logistics, meteorology, education) — all of which are accessible to PCM graduates through different entry routes.

## Entry Routes to AFA

### Route 1: NDA (Air Force Wing) — after Class 12
The most prestigious route to becoming an IAF pilot:

1. Clear NDA written exam (UPSC) + SSB interview
2. Opt for Air Force wing during NDA training at Khadakwasla (3 years)
3. Complete flying training at AFA Dundigal (1.5 years)
4. Commissioned as Flying Officer (Pilot)

**Critical requirement:** Perfect eyesight. NDA Air Force wing requires vision correctable to 6/6. Any color blindness is disqualifying. Height: minimum 162.5 cm.

### Route 2: AFCAT (Air Force Common Admission Test) — after graduation
The primary entry route for graduates:

1. **Eligibility:**
   - Flying Branch: Any Bachelor's degree with 60%+ AND Physics and Math at 10+2 level. Age: 20-24 years. Engineering graduates (age 20-26) also eligible
   - Technical Branch: Engineering degree from recognized university. Age: 20-26 years
   - Ground Duty: Any Bachelor's degree (varies by branch). Age: 20-26 years
2. **AFCAT exam:** Conducted twice a year (February and August). Tests: General Awareness, Verbal Ability, Numerical Ability, Reasoning
3. **AFSB (Air Force Selection Board) interview:** 5-day assessment similar to SSB
4. **CPSS (Computerised Pilot Selection System):** For Flying Branch only — a battery of computer-based tests measuring pilot aptitude, spatial orientation, multitasking, and psychomotor coordination. **CPSS can only be attempted ONCE in a lifetime.** If your child fails CPSS, they cannot reapply for Flying Branch through any route
5. **Medical examination:** Aviation medical standards are the strictest in the Indian Armed Forces
6. **Register at [afcat.cdac.in](https://afcat.cdac.in)**

### Route 3: NCC Special Entry (Air Wing)
- NCC Air Wing 'C' certificate holders with a Bachelor's degree
- Direct AFSB testing (no AFCAT written exam)
- Must meet all physical and medical requirements

## Training at AFA (What Your Child Will Experience)

### Flying Branch Training (74 weeks):
AFA's flying training is world-class and intensely demanding:

- **Stage 1 (Ground training):** Aerodynamics, navigation, meteorology, aircraft systems, air regulations, and survival training. Cadets learn everything about how aircraft work before they fly them
- **Stage 2 (Basic flying):** First solo flight on Pilatus PC-7 Mk.II turboprop trainer. This is one of the most significant moments in any pilot's life. Basic maneuvers, circuits, instrument flying, and formation flying
- **Stage 3 (Intermediate/Advanced flying):** Training on HAL Kiran jet trainer or HAL HTT-40. Aerobatics, night flying, tactical flying, weapon delivery, and combat maneuvers
- **Wings ceremony:** Graduates receive their coveted "Wings" badge — the symbol of a qualified IAF pilot. Family members attend this prestigious ceremony
- **After AFA:** Pilots proceed to Operational Conversion Units (OCUs) where they train on operational aircraft — Sukhoi Su-30MKI, Rafale, MiG-29, Tejas, Jaguar, or transport/helicopter aircraft depending on the stream

### Ground Duty/Technical Branch Training (52 weeks):
- Administrative training, technical specialization, leadership development
- Technical branch officers may train at AFTC (Air Force Technical College) Bangalore for specialized engineering courses

## Career Progression & Benefits

### IAF-specific benefits (beyond standard military benefits):
- **Flying Branch officers receive Flying Allowance** — additional ₹25,000-35,000/month on top of basic pay, depending on aircraft type
- **Test pilots** (top performers) get the most advanced aircraft and additional specialist training, often abroad
- **IAF officers can pilot civilian aircraft after retirement** — many become commercial airline pilots (with additional licensing), earning ₹30-80 LPA
- **Foreign postings and training:** IAF regularly sends officers for training exchanges with the US Air Force (USAF), Royal Air Force (RAF), and French Air Force
- **Retirement age:** Group Captain and below retire at 54 (later than Army). Higher ranks serve even longer

## Honest Reality Check

- **Flying Branch is extremely competitive.** From lakhs of applicants, only 100-150 pilots are selected per year through all entry routes combined. The CPSS test alone eliminates 60-70% of otherwise qualified candidates. If your child has their heart set on being a pilot, prepare them mentally for the possibility of not being selected — and have backup options ready
- **Vision standards are non-negotiable.** Laser eye surgery (LASIK/PRK) is NOT accepted for Flying Branch entry. If your child has significant vision problems, Ground Duty or Technical Branch are alternatives within the IAF
- **CPSS is once-in-a-lifetime.** Unlike NDA or AFCAT (which can be attempted multiple times), CPSS can only be taken ONCE. If your child fails, they permanently lose eligibility for Flying Branch. This makes preparation critical
- **Ground Duty and Technical Branch are excellent careers too.** Not everyone becomes a fighter pilot — but IAF officers in logistics, radar operations, missile systems, administration, and engineering have fulfilling careers with the same pay and benefits
