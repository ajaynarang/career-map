## Why India for Merchant Navy?

India is the world's third-largest supplier of merchant navy officers, after the Philippines and China. Indian maritime colleges — particularly TS Chanakya (Mumbai) and IMU campuses — are recognized globally, and Indian officers serve on ships operated by companies from Singapore, Hong Kong, Europe, and the Middle East.

**The salary reality is extraordinary:** A freshly graduated 3rd Officer or 5th Engineer (age 22-23) earns ₹50,000-80,000/month. A Chief Officer (age 30-35) earns ₹3-5 lakh/month. A Captain or Chief Engineer earns ₹5-10 lakh/month. And here's the key: if you spend 182+ days at sea in a financial year, your entire salary is **tax-free** under Indian income tax law. Very few careers offer this kind of income at this age.

**The cost is remarkably low:** Government maritime colleges cost ₹5-8L total for the full course. Even private academies cost ₹12-20L — still far less than engineering at a private college. The ROI is among the best of any career path.

## The Path

1. **Class 11-12:** Focus on PCM — Physics and Math are the foundation of marine engineering and navigation. Maintain 60%+ in PCM (minimum eligibility) but aim for 70%+.
2. **Get a medical checkup in Class 11:** Eyesight is a hard filter. Deck officers need 6/6 vision without glasses and normal color vision. Marine engineers have more relaxed vision requirements.
3. **IMU CET registration (October-November):** Apply at [imu.edu.in](https://www.imu.edu.in). The exam tests Physics, Chemistry, Math, English, and General Aptitude.
4. **IMU CET exam (May-June):** Performance determines counselling rank.
5. **Board exams (February-March):** Score 60%+ in PCM. Higher marks improve counselling rank.
6. **Counselling and allotment (June-July):** Choose between B.Sc Nautical Science and B.Tech Marine Engineering.
7. **Medical fitness test:** Strict DGMS (Director General of Medical Services) medical examination before joining.

## What Parents Should Know

- **Eyesight is a hard filter — check early.** For Nautical Science (deck officer path): 6/6 vision in each eye without glasses, normal color vision, no squint. For Marine Engineering (engine room): correctable to 6/12 acceptable. Get an eye checkup in Class 10 to plan accordingly.
- **Swimming is mandatory.** Your child must swim 50 meters in a pool during the medical/admission process. If they can't swim, start lessons immediately.
- **Ship life means months away from home.** Officers typically work 6-9 months on ship, then 2-4 months on leave. This cycle continues throughout the career. Some love the travel and adventure; others find the isolation challenging. Discuss this honestly with your child.
- **Only DG Shipping-approved colleges are legitimate.** The maritime industry has many unrecognized institutions. Verify any college's approval status at [dgshipping.gov.in](https://www.dgshipping.gov.in). Government colleges (IMU, TS Chanakya, MERI) and established private academies (Tolani, Anglo-Eastern) are safe bets.
- **Two career paths — choose wisely:**
  - **B.Sc Nautical Science (3 years + 18 months sea time):** Becomes a Deck Officer — navigation, cargo handling, ship operations. Requires perfect eyesight.
  - **B.Tech Marine Engineering (4 years + 6 months sea time):** Becomes an Engine Officer — engine room operations, maintenance, machinery. More relaxed vision requirements.
- **Physical fitness is genuinely important.** Ship work is physically demanding — climbing ladders, handling heavy equipment, working in engine rooms at 45°C+. Your child needs to be physically fit, not just academically strong.
- **Shore jobs exist after 8-10 years.** Many officers transition to shore-based roles: port management, shipping company operations, marine insurance, maritime law, classification societies. The sea experience commands premium salaries on shore (₹20-40 LPA).
- **Merchant Navy is NOT the Indian Navy.** There's no military service, no combat, no uniforms (beyond professional standards). It's a civilian commercial career on cargo ships, tankers, and container vessels.
