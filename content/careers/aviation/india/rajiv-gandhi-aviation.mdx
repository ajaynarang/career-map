## Why Rajiv Gandhi Academy?

Rajiv Gandhi Academy for Aviation Technology (RGAAT) is one of India's established private flying training institutes, located near Thiruvananthapuram International Airport in Kerala. Founded with the goal of providing quality pilot training in South India, RGAAT offers CPL training and, uniquely, an integrated B.Sc Aviation program that combines a university degree with flying training.

**For families in South India:** RGAAT provides a convenient option for students from Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh who don't want to relocate to Uttar Pradesh (IGRUA) or Maharashtra (BFC) for training. The Trivandrum location offers good weather for a significant portion of the year and proximity to an international airport.

**The integrated degree option:** RGAAT's B.Sc Aviation program (in partnership with a university) allows students to earn a Bachelor's degree alongside their CPL. This gives your child a fallback academic qualification — if the aviation career doesn't work out immediately (airline hiring freeze, medical issue), they have a degree to pursue other options.

## How to Get In

RGAAT offers rolling admissions with a straightforward process:

1. **Application:** Apply online at the academy's website or visit in person.
2. **Interview:** Personal interview assessing motivation, awareness of aviation career, and communication skills.
3. **DGCA Class 1 Medical:** Must be completed before starting flight training. Get examined at a DGCA-approved center.
4. **Document submission:** Class 12 marksheet (50% in PCM minimum), age proof, medical certificate.
5. **Fee agreement:** RGAAT typically requires an initial deposit with a structured payment schedule.

**No competitive entrance exam:** Unlike IGRUA, RGAAT does not have a competitive entrance process. This makes it more accessible, but it also means the academy accepts a wider range of students. Your child's success depends entirely on their dedication during training and performance in DGCA exams.

## Training Structure

**CPL Course (18-24 months typical):**

**Ground School:**
- Air Navigation — route planning, chart reading, GPS/VOR/NDB navigation
- Aviation Meteorology — weather systems, visibility, turbulence, flight planning
- Air Regulation — DGCA rules, airspace, radio telephony
- Technical General — aircraft systems, engines, instruments, aerodynamics
- DGCA CPL ground exams (four papers) — can be taken during training

**Flying Training:**
- **Single-engine training** — basic manoeuvres, circuits, solo flying, cross-country navigation, night flying
- **Multi-engine training** — twin-engine operations, engine failure procedures, instrument flying
- **Instrument Rating (IR)** — flying by instruments alone, mandatory for CPL
- Total: 200 hours minimum flying time
- Training aircraft: Cessna 152/172 (single-engine), multi-engine aircraft for advanced training

**B.Sc Aviation (Integrated Program — 3 years):**
- Combines CPL flying training with a Bachelor's degree in Aviation Science
- Academic coursework: aviation management, airport operations, airline economics, aviation safety
- Flying training runs parallel to academic semesters
- Graduate with both a CPL and a university degree

**Advantage of the integrated program:** If airline hiring is slow when your child graduates, the B.Sc degree allows them to work in airport management, airline operations, aviation consulting, or other aviation-adjacent roles while building flying hours.

## Weather & Training Conditions

Thiruvananthapuram's weather profile for flying training:

- **October-February:** Excellent flying weather — clear skies, good visibility. Peak training season.
- **March-May:** Hot but generally flyable. Some haze reduces visibility.
- **June-September:** Southwest monsoon — heavy rain, low clouds, limited flying. Expect reduced training days during these months.

**Training duration reality:** A "18-month" course at RGAAT typically takes 20-28 months due to monsoon delays, aircraft maintenance schedules, and DGCA exam timing. Budget time and finances accordingly.

## Life During Training

- **Residential option available** — RGAAT offers hostel accommodation for out-of-station trainees
- **Thiruvananthapuram** — a pleasant, safe city with excellent food, healthcare, and connectivity. The city is more affordable than Mumbai or Delhi
- **Airport proximity** — training at/near an international airport gives trainees exposure to real airline operations
- **Kerala's quality of life** — excellent healthcare, high literacy, clean environment. Parents visiting will find it comfortable
- **South Indian student community** — many trainees from Kerala, Tamil Nadu, and Karnataka. Cultural familiarity for South Indian families

## What to Consider

**Honest advice for parents evaluating RGAAT:**

1. **Compare with IGRUA first.** IGRUA costs ₹20-30 lakh less and has government credibility. If your child can get into IGRUA, that should be the first choice. RGAAT is a strong option if IGRUA seats are full or if the South India location is important.
2. **Verify DG CA approval status.** Always check the current DGCA approval at [dgca.gov.in](https://www.dgca.gov.in) before paying fees. Approval must be valid for the current year.
3. **Ask about aircraft availability.** Training speed depends on aircraft availability. Ask how many aircraft the academy has, their serviceability rate, and average monthly flying hours per student.
4. **The CPL is the CPL.** Regardless of where your child trains (IGRUA, BFC, RGAAT, or abroad), the DGCA CPL is the same qualification. Airlines don't differentiate between CPL holders based on training school once you're past the initial screening.
5. **Budget for post-CPL costs.** After CPL, budget ₹15-25 lakh for type rating (Boeing 737 or Airbus A320) and ₹5-10 lakh for the period between CPL and airline hiring. Total realistic investment: ₹75-100 lakh for the entire journey from Class 12 to first airline job.
