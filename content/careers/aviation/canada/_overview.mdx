## Why Canada for Aviation?

Canada offers a compelling middle ground for pilot training — better weather than the UK, clearer immigration than the US, and lower costs than both. Transport Canada CPL is globally respected and can be converted to DGCA, FAA, or other national licenses. Canada's aviation industry is growing, with carriers like WestJet, Air Canada, and Porter Airlines actively hiring.

**The immigration advantage:** This is Canada's biggest differentiator. If your child trains at a designated learning institution (DLI) — like Seneca College — they qualify for a Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) of up to 3 years. They can work as a pilot in Canada during this period, build hours, and eventually apply for Permanent Residency through Express Entry. No other English-speaking country offers this clear a pathway from pilot training to immigration.

**The cost picture:** CPL training in Canada costs ₹48-84L — cheaper than the UK (₹1-1.5 Crore) and comparable to the US. Combined degree + CPL programs at institutions like Seneca College cost more but provide both a diploma and flying credentials.

**The pilot shortage is real:** Canada has a severe pilot shortage, particularly in regional aviation and northern operations. This means job prospects for qualified pilots are strong, and employers are willing to sponsor work permits for skilled international graduates.

## The Path

1. **Class 11-12:** Focus on PCM. Prepare for IELTS (aim for 6.5+). Get a preliminary medical checkup.
2. **Research Canadian flight schools:** Seneca College (Toronto), Moncton Flight College (New Brunswick), Pacific Flying Club (Vancouver), Canadian Flyers International (Toronto).
3. **Apply to flight schools (January-March):** DLIs have specific intake dates. Apply early for September or January intake.
4. **Study Permit:** Apply for a Canadian Study Permit once you receive a letter of acceptance from a DLI.
5. **Transport Canada Medical:** Complete a Category 1 Aviation Medical Examination with a Transport Canada-designated Civil Aviation Medical Examiner (CAME).
6. **Begin training:** Ground school + flying. CPL requires 200 hours minimum flying time.
7. **After qualification:** Apply for PGWP. Work as a pilot or flight instructor in Canada. Build hours toward airline minimums.

## What Parents Should Know

- **Choose a DLI (Designated Learning Institution).** Only graduates of DLIs qualify for PGWP. Standalone flight schools that are not DLIs won't qualify your child for post-study work rights. Seneca College is a DLI. Many standalone flying clubs are not. Verify at the IRCC DLI list.
- **Transport Canada CPL requires 200 flying hours.** Same as DGCA. The training syllabus is comprehensive and well-structured.
- **Canadian winters affect flying.** November-March brings snow, ice, and low visibility in most of Canada. Training days are reduced during winter. However, winter flying training is uniquely valuable — Canadian pilots learn to handle weather that pilots in warmer countries never encounter.
- **Building hours in Canada is easier than in the US.** Canada has vast northern and remote communities served by small aircraft. Pilots with CPL can work as bush pilots, float plane pilots, or regional airline First Officers to build hours. These jobs are less competitive than in the US.
- **PGWP is the game-changer.** After completing a program at a DLI, your child gets up to 3 years of open work authorization. During this time, they can work as a pilot for any Canadian operator and build the hours needed for major airlines.
- **Conversion to DGCA is possible.** If your child returns to India, Transport Canada CPL can be converted to DGCA CPL by passing DGCA ground exams (similar to FAA conversion).
- **Canadian pilot salaries are strong.** Regional airline First Officers: CAD 50,000-75,000/year. Major airline Captains: CAD 150,000-250,000/year. Bush pilots and northern operators: CAD 40,000-60,000/year but with rapid hour-building.
