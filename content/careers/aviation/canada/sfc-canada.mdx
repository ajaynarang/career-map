## Why Seneca Aviation?

Seneca Polytechnic's School of Aviation (formerly Seneca College) is Canada's premier college-based aviation program. Located in Toronto — Canada's largest city and aviation hub — Seneca offers the rare combination of a recognized academic credential (Honours Bachelor degree or Diploma) with Transport Canada CPL flight training. This dual qualification is a strategic advantage that standalone flight schools cannot match.

**The critical advantage — PGWP eligibility:** Because Seneca is a Designated Learning Institution (DLI), graduates of its aviation programs qualify for Canada's Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP). The 4-year degree program qualifies for a 3-year PGWP. This means your child can work as a pilot in Canada for 3 years after graduation — building hours, earning income, and potentially qualifying for Permanent Residency. Standalone flight schools that are not DLIs cannot offer this pathway.

**For Indian families:** Seneca solves the two biggest problems in aviation — the employment gap (guaranteed work rights through PGWP) and the qualification risk (both a degree and a CPL). If your child can't find an airline job immediately, the Bachelor's degree in Aviation Technology opens doors to airline operations, airport management, aviation safety, and other aviation careers.

## How to Get In

1. **Apply through Ontario Colleges (OCAS)** for diploma programs or directly through Seneca for the Bachelor's program. Apply at [senecapolytechnic.ca](https://www.senecapolytechnic.ca/registrar/admission/international.html).
2. **Academic requirements:** Indian Class 12 with 65%+ overall. Strong marks in Math and Physics are essential.
3. **English requirement:** IELTS 6.5 overall (no band below 6.0) or TOEFL 84.
4. **Transport Canada Medical:** Complete a Category 1 Aviation Medical Examination with a Transport Canada-designated CAME (Civil Aviation Medical Examiner). This can be completed before or shortly after admission.
5. **Study Permit:** Apply for a Canadian Study Permit once you receive your Letter of Acceptance.

**Application timeline:**
- September intake: Apply by February-March (best for scholarship consideration)
- January intake: Apply by September-October
- Rolling admissions — but apply early, as aviation seats fill quickly

## Programs

**Honours Bachelor of Aviation Technology (4 years):**
- Transport Canada CPL + Multi-Engine + Instrument Rating
- Academic coursework: aviation management, human factors, flight safety, airline operations, aviation law
- Industry practicum: mandatory placement with a Canadian aviation company
- Total flying hours: 200+ hours
- PGWP eligibility: 3 years (maximum duration)
- **This is the recommended program for international students** — it maximizes PGWP duration and provides a degree alongside flight credentials

**Flight Services Diploma (2 years):**
- Transport Canada CPL + Multi-Engine + Instrument Rating
- Focused on flying with minimal academic coursework
- Faster completion but shorter PGWP eligibility (potentially 1-2 years depending on program length)
- Good option if cost is the primary concern

**Training aircraft:**
- Diamond DA20 (primary single-engine)
- Diamond DA42 (multi-engine)
- Modern glass cockpit aircraft — trains students on the same technology used in airline cockpits
- Seneca operates from two airfields: Buttonville Airport (north Toronto) and Peterborough Airport (dedicated training environment)

## The Career Pipeline in Canada

**Step 1: Graduate + PGWP (Year 0-1)**
- Apply for PGWP immediately after graduation (3-year duration for degree graduates)
- Apply to Canadian airlines and operators

**Step 2: Build Hours (Year 1-3)**
Options for building hours toward airline minimums:
- **Flight instructor at Seneca or other schools** — teach new students, build hours, earn income
- **Northern and remote operations** — Canadian North, First Air, and regional operators hire low-hour pilots for bush flying and remote community service. Hard work but rapid hour-building (500-1000 hours/year)
- **Charter and corporate aviation** — smaller operators with lower hour requirements

**Step 3: Regional Airlines (Year 2-4)**
At 500-1000 hours, apply to regional carriers: Jazz Aviation (Air Canada feeder), PAL Airlines, Calm Air, Bearskin Airlines. Starting salary: CAD 45,000-70,000/year.

**Step 4: Major Airlines (Year 5+)**
With 2000+ hours and regional experience, apply to Air Canada, WestJet, Porter Airlines. Captain salary: CAD 150,000-250,000/year.

**Step 5: Permanent Residency**
After 1-2 years of skilled work during PGWP, apply for PR through Express Entry or Provincial Nominee Program. Pilots are in demand — strong CRS (Comprehensive Ranking System) scores.

## Life in Toronto During Training

- **Toronto** — Canada's largest and most multicultural city. Massive Indian community, Indian grocery stores, temples, and cultural events everywhere
- **Two training campuses** — Buttonville (within Toronto metro) and Peterborough (1.5 hours northeast, dedicated training airspace with less congestion)
- **Weather reality** — Toronto has cold winters (November-March, -10°C to -25°C). Flying continues in winter but at a reduced pace. This is actually valuable training — learning to fly in Canadian winter conditions makes your child a more capable pilot
- **Living costs** — Toronto is Canada's most expensive city. Budget CAD 1,200-1,800/month for a shared apartment. Peterborough campus area is significantly cheaper
- **Part-time work** — students at DLIs can work 20 hours/week during semester. At Ontario's minimum wage (CAD 16.55/hour), this provides useful supplementary income
