## Why ANU for Science?

ANU is Australia's #1 university for physics, astronomy, and earth sciences — and it's not close. Located in Canberra (Australia's capital), ANU was explicitly created to be a national research university, and science is at its core. The Research School of Physics produced Nobel laureate Brian Schmidt (2011 Physics Nobel for discovering the accelerating expansion of the universe). The Research School of Astronomy and Astrophysics operates the Siding Spring Observatory — the premier optical observatory in the Southern Hemisphere.

What makes ANU unique for science: **direct access to world-class research facilities from the undergraduate level**. ANU's telescopes, quantum optics labs, nuclear physics facilities, and earth science field stations are available to undergraduates for research projects. The student-to-researcher ratio is one of the best in the world — ANU has more researchers per student than any other Australian university.

**The Canberra advantage for cost:** While Sydney and Melbourne are expensive, Canberra is significantly more affordable — rent is 20-30% lower, and the city is compact and bikeable. ANU's total cost (₹36-39L/year) is substantially lower than equivalent universities in Sydney or Melbourne, and competitive with many Indian private universities.

**The astronomy factor:** If your child is fascinated by the cosmos, ANU is one of the top 5 places in the world to study astronomy. Access to the southern sky, world-class telescopes, and a department that includes Nobel laureates and leaders in exoplanet research and cosmology — this combination is available at very few institutions globally.

## How to Get In

ANU admission is straightforward and grades-based.

**Requirements:**
- **Class 12 board scores:** 80%+ for standard admission; 85%+ for scholarship consideration
- **English proficiency:** IELTS 6.5+ or TOEFL 80+
- **No entrance exam or interview** — direct admission based on academic record

**Application timeline:**
- Semester 1 (February start): Apply by December 15 (recommended) — offers continue until January
- Semester 2 (July start): Apply by May 15
- Applications through [anu.edu.au/study/apply](https://www.anu.edu.au/study/apply)

**The ANU BSc is flexible:** Students don't have to declare a major immediately. First-year students take courses across multiple sciences before specializing. This is ideal for students who are interested in science broadly but haven't committed to a specific discipline.

## Curriculum & Structure

3-year Bachelor of Science (BSc) + optional 1-year Honours.

- **Year 1:** Broad science foundation. Choose from physics, math, chemistry, biology, earth sciences, and astronomy. No commitment to a major required
- **Year 2:** Begin specialization. Core courses in your chosen major + electives
- **Year 3:** Advanced courses + capstone project or research component
- **Honours year (optional but recommended):** A 4th year consisting primarily of an original research thesis supervised by a faculty member. Essential for PhD admission

### Key Programs

- **Physics:** Classical and quantum mechanics, electrodynamics, statistical physics, condensed matter, nuclear physics. Access to ANU's Research School of Physics facilities
- **Astronomy and Astrophysics:** Observational and theoretical astronomy, cosmology, planetary science. Students use the 2.3m telescope at Siding Spring Observatory and participate in observing runs
- **Mathematics:** Pure and applied math, with strength in mathematical physics and computational mathematics
- **Chemistry:** Organic, inorganic, physical, and environmental chemistry. Modern labs in the Research School of Chemistry
- **Quantum Technology:** A new and unique program combining quantum physics, engineering, and computer science — preparing students for the emerging quantum computing industry
- **Earth Sciences:** Geology, geophysics, geochemistry. Field trips to Australia's unique geological formations (Canberra is surrounded by ancient geological features)

## Research & Labs

ANU's research infrastructure for a university of its size is exceptional:

- **Research School of Astronomy and Astrophysics (RSAA)** — operates Siding Spring Observatory (Coonabarabran, NSW) with multiple telescopes including the 2.3m Advanced Technology Telescope. Undergraduate students participate in observing campaigns
- **Research School of Physics** — nuclear physics (14UD tandem accelerator), quantum optics, laser physics, plasma physics, and photovoltaics. Nobel laureate Brian Schmidt is based here
- **ANU Centre for Quantum Computation and Communication Technology** — one of the world's leading quantum computing research centers. The Quantum Technology BSc feeds directly into this research
- **Research School of Chemistry** — modern synthetic, analytical, and computational chemistry facilities
- **Research School of Earth Sciences** — SHRIMP (Sensitive High-Resolution Ion Microprobe) — a world-famous instrument for dating geological samples, invented at ANU
- **Summer Research Scholarships:** ANU offers paid summer research positions (AUD $500-600/week) for undergraduates. Highly recommended as preparation for Honours and PhD

## Campus Life

- **Canberra is a planned city** — clean, green, safe, and compact. Population 470,000. The university is a 5-minute bike ride from the city center
- **ANU campus** — 145 hectares of parkland, modern buildings, and historic colleges. Sullivans Creek runs through campus. Kangaroos occasionally appear on the grounds
- **Affordable living** — Canberra rent is AUD $200-350/week for shared housing. Cheaper than Sydney (AUD $350-500) or Melbourne (AUD $300-450)
- **Indian community** — Canberra has a growing Indian population. Indian grocery stores, restaurants, and temples are available. The ANU Indian Students' Association hosts regular events
- **On-campus residences** — 7 residential halls and colleges. First-year students are prioritized. Includes meal plans and social programs
- **Outdoor lifestyle** — Canberra is surrounded by national parks, mountains, and bushland. Hiking, mountain biking, and skiing (Thredbo/Perisher, 2 hours away) are popular
- **Government city** — Canberra is home to Parliament House, national museums, and government agencies (CSIRO, Bureau of Meteorology, Geoscience Australia). Science policy and government research internships are uniquely accessible
- **Climate:** Warm summers (25-35 C), cold winters (0-10 C, occasionally below 0). More distinct seasons than Sydney or Melbourne, but milder than Canadian or Northern European winters
