## Why Chennai Mathematical Institute?

CMI is India's answer to the Ecole Normale Superieure (Paris) or the Institute for Advanced Study (Princeton) — a tiny, elite institution laser-focused on mathematics and theoretical computer science. If your child loves mathematics for its own sake — the beauty of proofs, the elegance of abstract structures — CMI is where they belong.

What makes CMI extraordinary: a student-to-faculty ratio that's almost 1:1 in some programs, faculty who are Fields Medal committee members and fellows of international academies, and an intellectual culture where mathematics is not a means to an end but the end itself. CMI graduates are among the most sought-after candidates for PhD programs at Princeton, MIT, Harvard, and the University of Chicago.

**The career surprise:** CMI graduates who choose industry over academia are recruited aggressively by quantitative finance firms. Goldman Sachs, Tower Research Capital, Jane Street, and DE Shaw actively recruit from CMI. Starting salaries of ₹20-40 LPA are common for quant roles. The mathematical maturity CMI builds is precisely what these firms value.

## How to Get In

CMI has its own entrance exam — independent of JEE, IAT, or any other national exam.

**CMI Entrance Exam:**
- **Format:** 3-hour exam with two parts. Part A is objective (math). Part B has subjective proof-based problems. For Math & CS, there's an additional CS section. For Math & Physics, a physics section
- **Style:** Olympiad-style problems testing mathematical reasoning, not computation. If your child enjoys RMO/INMO problems, they'll find this exam natural
- **When:** Usually May-June. Applications open in March
- **Seats:** Only 50-60 students admitted per year across all BSc programs

**What CMI looks for:**
- Mathematical maturity beyond the school syllabus
- Ability to write clear, rigorous proofs
- Olympiad background (RMO, INMO qualifiers have an edge, but it's not mandatory)

**Reality check:** CMI's entrance exam is among India's toughest — not because the syllabus is vast, but because the problems require deep thinking. Students who memorize formulas struggle; students who understand mathematics thrive.

## Curriculum & Structure

3-year BSc (Hons) — one of the few 3-year pure science programs in India worth pursuing.

- **Mathematics core:** Algebra, Analysis, Topology, Differential Geometry, Number Theory — taught at a level comparable to top international programs
- **Math & CS track:** Adds algorithms, complexity theory, programming (Haskell, functional programming emphasis) — produces graduates equally comfortable in pure math and theoretical CS
- **Math & Physics track:** Adds classical mechanics, quantum mechanics, statistical physics — ideal for students interested in mathematical physics
- **Small class sizes:** 20-30 students per class. Every student is known by name to every professor
- **Seminar culture:** Students present papers, discuss open problems, and attend research talks from visiting mathematicians regularly
- **No rote learning:** Exams are proof-based and open-ended. Understanding is the only currency that matters
- **Research exposure:** By the 3rd year, most students are reading research papers and some begin contributing to ongoing research

## Campus Life

CMI's campus in Siruseri (IT Corridor, 40 km south of Chennai city center) is small but functional.

- **Intimate community** — 50-60 students per batch means extremely close bonds with peers and faculty. Dinner conversations about unsolved math problems are normal
- **Faculty accessibility** — professors' doors are always open. It's common for students to walk into a professor's office with a problem and spend an hour discussing it
- **Library** — specialized mathematics library with journals and monographs you won't find at most Indian institutions
- **Hostel** — on-campus accommodation available, though limited. Many students share apartments nearby
- **Chennai** — warm climate year-round. Rich cultural heritage (music, temples, South Indian cuisine). More affordable than Bangalore or Mumbai
- **Annual events** — Tessellate (math fest), invited lecture series, and workshops featuring international mathematicians
- **The CMI network** — small but powerful. CMI alumni are professors at top global universities, quant researchers at hedge funds, and leaders in India's mathematics community
