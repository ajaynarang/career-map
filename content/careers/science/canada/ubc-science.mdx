## Why UBC for Science?

UBC sits on a stunning 400-hectare campus on the western tip of Vancouver, overlooking the Pacific Ocean and the Coast Mountains. It's consistently ranked Canada's #2 university and is globally top-30 for physics, environmental sciences, and earth sciences. But what truly distinguishes UBC for science is its unique combination of academic excellence, natural environment, and research facilities.

**The TRIUMF connection:** UBC is home to TRIUMF — Canada's national particle accelerator centre and one of the world's leading subatomic physics labs. TRIUMF produces medical isotopes, conducts fundamental particle physics research, and develops technologies for nuclear medicine. UBC physics undergraduates can do research at TRIUMF — an opportunity available at very few universities globally.

**Environmental and Earth Sciences:** UBC's location on the Pacific Rim, near old-growth rainforests, active volcanoes, and marine ecosystems, makes it one of the world's best places to study environmental science, earth sciences, and oceanography. The Pacific Museum of Earth, the Beaty Biodiversity Museum, and UBC's own research forests are teaching and research resources right on campus.

**The Vancouver advantage:** Vancouver is consistently ranked among the world's most livable cities. Mild climate (no brutal winters like Toronto), stunning natural beauty, and a large Indian diaspora (Surrey, a neighboring city, is heavily Punjabi). UBC graduates benefit from Vancouver's growing tech sector and proximity to Seattle (2.5 hours south), home to Amazon, Microsoft, and Boeing.

## How to Get In

UBC admission is primarily grades-based with a "Personal Profile" component.

**Requirements:**
- **Class 12 board scores:** 87%+ recommended in PCM. Higher for scholarships
- **English proficiency:** IELTS 6.5+ or TOEFL 90+
- **Personal Profile:** Short essays about activities, experiences, and goals. Not as intensive as US essays, but thoughtful responses matter
- **No SAT/ACT required**

**Application timeline:**
- Applications open: October 1
- Deadline: January 15 (for September start)
- Offers: February-April (rolling)
- Scholarship decisions: April

**Co-op option:** UBC Science offers co-op programs where students alternate study terms with paid work terms. Available in Physics, Chemistry, Math, Statistics, and other departments. Co-op students earn CAD $15,000-25,000 per work term and graduate with 12-16 months of professional experience.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year Bachelor of Science (BSc).

- **First year:** Broad science foundation. Students take courses in multiple sciences and math before declaring a specialization at the end of Year 1. This flexibility is valuable for students unsure whether they prefer physics, math, or earth sciences
- **Years 2-3:** Core courses in your specialization + electives
- **Year 4:** Advanced courses + Honours thesis (for Honours program students)
- **Honours vs. Major:** "Honours" programs are more intensive, include a research thesis, and are recommended for students planning PhD studies. "Major" programs are less demanding and allow more elective freedom
- **Combined Honours:** UBC allows combining two disciplines — e.g., Combined Honours in Physics and Mathematics, or Chemistry and Earth Sciences. This is excellent for interdisciplinary thinkers

### Key Programs

- **Physics:** Quantum mechanics, electrodynamics, statistical mechanics, astrophysics, condensed matter. Strong experimental and computational components. TRIUMF research from Year 2 onwards
- **Mathematics:** Pure math, applied math, and mathematical biology tracks. Strong in differential equations, algebra, and mathematical physics
- **Earth, Ocean and Atmospheric Sciences (EOAS):** Geology, geophysics, oceanography, atmospheric science. Field trips to BC's mountains, coastline, and volcanic regions are part of the curriculum
- **Environmental Sciences:** Interdisciplinary program combining ecology, chemistry, physics, and policy. Ideal for students interested in climate change, conservation, and sustainability
- **Astronomy:** One of Canada's few standalone undergraduate astronomy programs. Access to UBC's observatories and collaboration with NRC Herzberg

## Research & Labs

- **TRIUMF** — Canada's national particle accelerator lab, on the UBC campus. Nuclear and particle physics, medical isotopes, accelerator science. Undergrads can join research teams
- **Stewart Blusson Quantum Matter Institute** — quantum materials, topological insulators, superconductors. World-class condensed matter research
- **Pacific Institute for the Mathematical Sciences (PIMS)** — promotes mathematical research and education across western Canada. UBC is a lead institution
- **EOAS Research Labs** — seismology, volcanology, oceanography. Field stations on Vancouver Island and in the Coast Mountains
- **Chemistry Labs** — modern facilities in the Chemistry Building. Strong in materials chemistry, catalysis, and environmental chemistry
- **Undergraduate Research Awards (URA):** Paid summer research positions ($6,000-8,000) for undergraduates working with faculty

## Campus Life

- **The campus is spectacular** — 400 hectares on a peninsula surrounded by forest and ocean. Wreck Beach (clothing-optional), Pacific Spirit Park trails, and mountain views from campus
- **Vancouver weather is mild** — rainy (October-March) but temperatures rarely drop below 0 C. The mildest major city in Canada. Mountains, ocean, and ski hills within 30 minutes
- **Indian community is large** — Surrey and surrounding areas have one of the largest Punjabi communities outside India. Gurdwaras, Indian grocery stores, and restaurants are plentiful. UBC has active Indian student groups
- **Residence:** First-year students have priority for on-campus housing. UBC residences are modern and well-maintained. After Year 1, students move off-campus (rent: CAD $800-1,200/month in shared apartments)
- **Student life** — 350+ clubs and organizations. Science students have dedicated societies (Physics Society, Math Club, Chemistry Society)
- **Outdoor activities** — skiing at Whistler (2 hours), hiking in the North Shore mountains, kayaking, and surfing on Tofino's beaches. UBC Outdoor Club is one of the most popular student groups
- **U-Pass** — student transit pass included in fees, giving unlimited public transit across Metro Vancouver
