## Why Cambridge for Science?

Cambridge isn't just a university — it's the place where modern science was invented. Isaac Newton developed calculus and the laws of motion here. James Clerk Maxwell wrote the equations of electromagnetism here. J.J. Thomson discovered the electron, Rutherford split the atom, Crick and Watson discovered DNA's structure, and Stephen Hawking worked on black holes — all at Cambridge. The Cavendish Laboratory alone has produced 30 Nobel Prize winners.

What makes Cambridge unique for science students: the **Natural Sciences Tripos**. Unlike every other UK university where you must choose one specific science at age 18, Cambridge lets you study **multiple sciences** simultaneously. In your first year, you choose 3 subjects from Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Materials Science, Earth Sciences, and Evolution & Behaviour, plus a math course. You specialize from Year 2 onwards. This means your child doesn't have to decide between physics and chemistry at 17 — they can explore and then commit.

**The supervision system:** Cambridge's secret weapon. Every week, your child meets with an expert (often a leading researcher) in groups of 1-3 students to discuss problems, review essays, and go deep into the material. This one-on-one teaching is available nowhere else at scale. It's the reason Cambridge produces so many exceptional scientists.

## How to Get In

Cambridge Natural Sciences is competitive — about 3,000 applicants for 550 places.

**The application process:**
1. **UCAS application by October 15** — Cambridge's early deadline
2. **ESAT (Engineering and Science Admissions Test)** — standardized test introduced in 2025 testing mathematical and scientific reasoning. Register separately at [cambridgeassessment.org.uk](https://www.cambridgeassessment.org.uk)
3. **College choice** — apply to a specific college (or make an "open application"). Each college admits independently. Some colleges (Trinity, St John's) are more competitive; others (newer colleges) may be slightly easier to get into
4. **Interview (November-December)** — the most critical part. Cambridge interviews all shortlisted candidates. For Indian students, interviews are conducted online. Expect to be given unfamiliar scientific problems and asked to think through them in real-time. Preparation tip: practice explaining your reasoning out loud
5. **Conditional offer** — typically A*A*A at A-Level or 95%+ in Indian boards

**Reality check:** Cambridge is looking for students who think like scientists — who can apply fundamental principles to unfamiliar situations, who ask "why?" and "what if?", and who show genuine intellectual curiosity beyond the syllabus.

**For Mathematics:** The application uses the MAT (Mathematics Admissions Test) instead of ESAT. Math at Cambridge is intensely competitive — about 1,800 applicants for 250 places.

## Curriculum & Structure

### Natural Sciences Tripos (3-year BA or 4-year MSci)

- **Year 1 (Part IA):** Choose 3 experimental sciences from: Physics, Chemistry, Biology of Cells, Physiology of Organisms, Materials Science, Earth Sciences, Evolution & Behaviour. Plus Mathematics for Natural Scientists
- **Year 2 (Part IB):** Narrow to 2-3 subjects. Options include Astrophysics, History & Philosophy of Science, and Biochemistry in addition to the core sciences
- **Year 3 (Part II):** Full specialization in ONE subject. This is where deep expertise is built. A research project or extended essay is required
- **Year 4 (Part III / MSci):** Advanced topics and a substantial research project. Part III Mathematics is legendarily difficult and is one of the best preparation grounds for PhD-level research in the world

### Mathematics Tripos
- 3-year BA + optional Part III (4th year MSci)
- Years 1-2: Pure math, applied math, statistics, mathematical physics
- Year 3: Choose from pure math, applied math, or theoretical physics
- Part III Mathematics: The most advanced taught mathematics course in the world. Students from around the globe apply to do just Part III

### Teaching & Assessment
- **Supervisions:** 1-3 students per supervisor, weekly. This is the heart of Cambridge education
- **Lectures:** Large-group teaching for content delivery
- **Practicals:** Extensive lab work for experimental sciences (Cavendish Laboratory for physics, Department of Chemistry labs)
- **Tripos exams:** End-of-year exams determine your class (First, 2:1, 2:2). Minimal continuous assessment

## Research & Labs

- **Cavendish Laboratory** — the most famous physics lab in the world. 30 Nobel Prizes. Now in a state-of-the-art facility on the West Cambridge site. Undergraduates do practical work here
- **Department of Chemistry** — world-class facilities for organic, inorganic, physical, and theoretical chemistry. Nobel laureates on the current faculty
- **Institute of Astronomy** — operates telescopes and leads research in exoplanets, cosmology, and stellar astrophysics
- **Cambridge Centre for Data-Driven Discovery** — computational science and data analytics research
- **Isaac Newton Institute for Mathematical Sciences** — hosts visiting researchers and programs in all areas of mathematics
- **Kavli Institute for Cosmology** — dark energy, dark matter, early universe research

## Campus Life

- **College life is everything** — your child will eat, sleep, study, socialize, and receive supervisions within their college. Each of Cambridge's 31 colleges has its own dining hall, library, gardens, and traditions
- **Indian community is strong** — Cambridge University Indian Society (CUIS) is one of the largest cultural societies. Diwali celebration in the Guildhall, Holi on the Backs, regular socials
- **Formal Halls** — sit-down dinners in medieval halls, wearing academic gowns. A unique and memorable tradition
- **Punting on the Cam** — the quintessential Cambridge experience
- **The city is small and safe** — cycle everywhere. Cambridge is one of the safest cities in England
- **Academically intense** — 8-week terms mean a fast pace. 3 terms per year. The workload is heavy
- **May Balls** — elaborate end-of-year celebrations hosted by colleges (held in June, confusingly)
- **London is 50 minutes by train** — for weekends, internships, and cultural activities
