## Why UChicago for Science?

The University of Chicago has a unique intellectual culture — it's where the nuclear age began (Enrico Fermi's first nuclear reactor was built under the football stadium in 1942), where modern economics was invented, and where the boundaries between science, mathematics, and philosophy are deliberately blurred. UChicago doesn't produce narrow specialists; it produces thinkers who see connections across disciplines.

What makes UChicago special for science: the **Core Curriculum**. Every student — regardless of major — takes a rigorous sequence of courses in humanities, social sciences, sciences, mathematics, and civilization studies. A Physics major at UChicago will read Plato, debate economic theory, and study art history. This isn't distraction from science; UChicago believes it makes better scientists.

**The Citadel connection:** Citadel, one of the world's most important quantitative finance firms, is headquartered in Chicago and recruits heavily from UChicago. Physics and Mathematics graduates from UChicago regularly receive offers of $150,000-$300,000 starting compensation in quant roles. The pipeline from UChicago science to quantitative finance is one of the strongest in the world.

## How to Get In

UChicago's acceptance rate is **5.2%**. What distinguishes UChicago from MIT or Caltech: the essays.

**UChicago's famous "uncommon essays"** are unlike any other college application:
- Past prompts include: "Where have all the flowers gone?" and "What's so odd about odd numbers?"
- These essays test creative and rigorous thinking — exactly what UChicago values
- Science applicants who can write beautifully about scientific ideas have a strong advantage

**What UChicago looks for in science applicants:**
- **Intellectual curiosity beyond your field** — a physicist who reads philosophy, a chemist who studies history
- **Research experience** — summer projects, publications, or meaningful investigations
- **Academic rigor** — the most challenging courses available, with top performance
- **Unconventional thinking** — UChicago values students who question assumptions

**Timeline:**
- Early Decision I: November 1 (binding)
- Early Decision II: January 2 (binding)
- Regular Decision: January 2
- Decisions: Mid-March (ED I: mid-December)

**Financial aid note:** UChicago is **need-aware for international students** — applying for aid can affect admission chances. However, their aid is generous, and merit scholarships (University Scholar Award = full tuition) are available for exceptional applicants.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year Bachelor of Science (SB) or Bachelor of Arts (AB).

- **Core Curriculum:** 15+ courses across humanities, social sciences, sciences, math, and civilization studies. This is about one-third of your total coursework. UChicago students are broadly educated — period
- **Physics:** One of the world's top departments. Strong in particle physics, cosmology, condensed matter, and AMO (atomic, molecular, optical) physics. Close ties to Fermilab and Argonne National Lab
- **Mathematics:** Deeply rigorous, proof-heavy, and theoretical. The department has produced 6 Fields Medalists. Analysis, algebra, and geometry courses are taught at a graduate level
- **Statistics:** UChicago essentially invented modern statistics. The Department of Statistics offers an outstanding undergraduate program with connections to data science and machine learning
- **Chemistry:** Nobel Prize-winning department. Strong in physical chemistry, organic chemistry, and chemical biology
- **Quarter system:** UChicago runs 4 quarters per year (vs. 2 semesters). Courses move fast — you take 3-4 courses per 10-week quarter. The pace is intense but allows you to take more total courses than at semester-based schools

## Research & Labs

- **Fermilab (Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory)** — the premier US particle physics lab, located 30 miles west of campus. UChicago manages Fermilab and undergrads can do research there
- **Argonne National Laboratory** — UChicago-managed national lab with facilities in materials science, chemistry, nuclear physics, and computing. Undergrad research opportunities available
- **Enrico Fermi Institute** — interdisciplinary research center for physics, astrophysics, and related fields
- **Kavli Institute for Cosmological Physics** — dark matter, dark energy, cosmic microwave background research
- **James Franck Institute** — world-renowned for condensed matter physics and materials science
- **Research for undergrads:** The Jeff Metcalf Program provides paid research internships. The College Research Fellows program supports independent research

## Campus Life

UChicago's Gothic campus in Hyde Park (South Side of Chicago) is architecturally stunning — often compared to Oxford and Cambridge.

- **"Where fun goes to die"** — the tongue-in-cheek motto reflects UChicago's unapologetically intellectual culture. Students here genuinely enjoy late-night debates about philosophy, physics, and politics
- **Scav Hunt** — the world's largest scavenger hunt, a 4-day UChicago tradition involving building items, performing stunts, and solving puzzles. Think of it as IIT tech fests on steroids
- **House system:** First and second-year students live in residential houses within larger dormitories. Each house has its own identity and traditions
- **Indian community** — Organization of South Asian Students (OSAS) is active. Chicago has a large Indian diaspora in Devon Avenue neighborhood ("Little India")
- **Chicago is a world-class city** — architecture, deep-dish pizza, jazz, Lake Michigan. A significant upgrade from small college towns
- **Weather warning:** Chicago winters are brutal — temperatures regularly drop to -15 to -20 C with "wind chill." The nickname "Windy City" is earned
