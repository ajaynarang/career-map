## Why Columbia for Science?

Columbia is the only Ivy League university in New York City — and for a science student, that location is transformative. The physics department produced 31 Nobel laureates, including Enrico Fermi, I.I. Rabi, and Tsung-Dao Lee. The first nuclear chain reaction was conceived here. The mathematics department is home to Fields Medalists and is one of the strongest in the world for pure and applied math.

**What makes Columbia special for science:** the combination of a world-class research university with unmatched access to New York City's intellectual and professional ecosystem. Your child can take a class on quantum field theory in the morning and attend a seminar at the Simons Foundation (world's largest private math/physics funder) in the afternoon. Brookhaven National Laboratory is 60 miles east on Long Island. The American Museum of Natural History is literally next door to campus.

**The financial reality is encouraging.** Columbia is **need-blind for international students** and meets 100% of demonstrated financial need. Families earning under $66,000/year pay nothing — including room, board, and books. This makes Columbia potentially more affordable than many private Indian universities.

## How to Get In

Columbia's acceptance rate is **3.9%** — among the lowest in the world. The admissions process is highly holistic.

**What Columbia looks for:**
- **Intellectual vitality** — Columbia's Core Curriculum is famous. They want students who are genuinely excited about reading great books, discussing philosophy, and thinking across disciplines — even as science majors
- **Academic excellence** — near-perfect grades and test scores are the baseline
- **Research or deep engagement** — published papers, science fair victories, or sustained independent exploration of a scientific question
- **Why Columbia, specifically** — the supplemental essays must show genuine understanding of Columbia's programs, culture, and NYC location

**Timeline:**
- Early Decision: November 1 (binding — higher acceptance rate, ~10%)
- Regular Decision: January 1
- Decisions: Late March

**Tip for Indian applicants:** Columbia's Core Curriculum is central to its identity. Your child's application should show enthusiasm for interdisciplinary learning — not just science, but literature, philosophy, and art. Columbia rejects science students who seem narrowly focused.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year Bachelor of Arts (BA) or Bachelor of Science (BS). Columbia offers both through Columbia College (BA) or the School of Engineering (BS in applied sciences).

- **Core Curriculum:** ALL Columbia College students take Literature Humanities, Contemporary Civilization, Art Humanities, Music Humanities, Frontiers of Science, and University Writing. This is non-negotiable and takes up roughly one-third of your coursework. It makes Columbia scientists unusually well-rounded
- **Physics:** Exceptional strength in particle physics, condensed matter, AMO (atomic, molecular, optical), and astrophysics. The department operates experiments at CERN, Brookhaven, and the South Pole
- **Mathematics:** One of the strongest departments in the US. Topology, algebraic geometry, number theory, probability, and mathematical physics. Close ties with the Simons Foundation and MSRI
- **Applied Physics:** Unique program combining physics with engineering applications — quantum computing, photonics, plasma physics, and materials science
- **Chemistry:** Strong in organic synthesis, chemical biology, and materials chemistry. The Havemeyer Hall labs are historic and state-of-the-art
- **Research from day one:** Columbia's undergraduate research programs connect freshmen with faculty labs. Many physics students join research groups in their first year

## Research & Labs

- **Nevis Laboratories** — Columbia's particle physics research facility. Major contributions to ATLAS at CERN and the MicroBooNE neutrino experiment
- **Columbia Astrophysics Lab** — X-ray astronomy, gravitational waves, and cosmological surveys
- **Pupin Physics Laboratories** — historic building where the Manhattan Project began. Now houses condensed matter, AMO, and theoretical physics groups
- **Northwest Corner Building** — interdisciplinary research at the intersection of science and engineering. Nanotechnology, systems biology, and quantum information
- **Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory** — world-leading earth science and climate research facility on the Hudson River

## Campus Life

- **Morningside Heights, Manhattan** — Columbia's campus is a green oasis in upper Manhattan, adjacent to Harlem and the Upper West Side. The neighborhood is safe, walkable, and culturally rich
- **New York City is the campus:** Museums, concerts, Broadway, international cuisine — all accessible by subway. For an intellectually curious student, NYC is the greatest classroom in the world
- **Indian community is extensive** — Columbia's South Asian population is large and active. Diwali on campus is a major celebration. NYC's Jackson Heights (Queens) is the largest Indian neighborhood outside India
- **Vegetarian-friendly** — NYC has hundreds of Indian and vegetarian restaurants. Columbia dining has extensive vegetarian options
- **Core Curriculum social life** — because all students take the same Core classes, there's a shared intellectual culture. Dinner conversations about Plato and quantum mechanics happen naturally
- **Cost of living is high** — NYC is expensive. Columbia provides housing for all four years, which helps control costs
- **Weather:** NYC has real winters (December-February below 0°C) and hot summers. Spring and fall are beautiful
