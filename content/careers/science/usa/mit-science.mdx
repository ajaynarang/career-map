## Why MIT for Science?

MIT isn't just the world's #1 engineering school — it's also the world's #1 for Physics and Chemistry, and top-3 for Mathematics. The MIT School of Science is where quantum computing was born (Peter Shor's algorithm), where the Higgs boson detection methods were developed, and where LIGO detected gravitational waves. Your child won't just learn science — they'll be learning from the people who are defining it.

What makes MIT unique for science: the **UROP (Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program)**. Over 85% of MIT undergraduates participate in research with faculty — many starting in their freshman year. This isn't busy work; MIT undergraduates routinely co-author papers in Nature, Science, and Physical Review Letters. A Physics major at MIT gets more research experience by graduation than most PhD students at other universities.

**The financial reality:** MIT is **need-blind for international students** and meets 100% of demonstrated need. If your family earns under $75,000/year (approximately ₹63 LPA), your child pays nothing. MIT may be cheaper than many Indian private colleges for middle-class families.

## How to Get In

MIT's overall acceptance rate is **3.9%**. For science-focused Indian applicants, the profile that gets admitted typically includes:

- **International Olympiad medals** (IPhO, IMO, IChO) — this is the strongest signal
- **Published research** — a genuine paper (even as a co-author) in a recognized journal
- **Exceptional science projects** — Google Science Fair winners, Intel ISEF finalists, or research with a university professor
- **Near-perfect academics** — SAT 1550+, near-perfect school grades
- **The "match"** — MIT wants curious builders and thinkers, not just exam toppers

**Timeline:**
- Early Action: November 1 (recommended; non-binding)
- Regular Action: January 1
- Decisions: Mid-March (EA: mid-December)

**Reality check:** Indian science students admitted to MIT almost always have International Olympiad participation or equivalent-level achievements. JEE preparation alone is not sufficient — MIT values breadth and depth of scientific curiosity.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year Bachelor of Science. MIT uses a numbered course system.

- **Course 8 (Physics):** One of the world's top physics programs. Tracks include theoretical physics, experimental physics, and physics with electrical engineering (Course 8-A). The department produced 8 Nobel laureates
- **Course 18 (Mathematics):** Pure math, applied math, and math with CS (Course 18-C). The department is legendary — faculty include Fields Medalists and Abel Prize winners
- **Course 5 (Chemistry):** Strong in physical chemistry, organic chemistry, and chemical biology. Deep connections to biotech industry
- **First year is pass/no-record** (fall semester) — eases the transition
- **GIRs (General Institute Requirements):** All students take 8 core subjects including physics, math, chemistry, and biology — plus humanities electives
- **UROP from day one:** Students can join research labs in their first semester. Paid or for credit
- **Cross-registration with Harvard** — take courses at Harvard (15 minutes away)
- **IAP (Independent Activities Period):** January term with workshops, short courses, and research intensives

## Research & Labs

This is where MIT science truly shines:

- **MIT Kavli Institute for Astrophysics and Space Research** — gravitational waves, exoplanets, X-ray astronomy
- **MIT Center for Theoretical Physics** — string theory, quantum field theory, quantum information
- **Koch Institute for Integrative Cancer Research** — where chemistry meets biology
- **MIT Lincoln Laboratory** — federally funded R&D center for advanced technology
- **Nuclear Reactor Lab** — yes, MIT has its own nuclear reactor, and undergraduates can use it for research

## Campus Life

- **Indian community is strong** — 200+ Indian international students, active Indian Students Association (ISA) and Sangam
- **Nerd culture celebrated** — problem sets, hackathons, and late-night discussions are the norm
- **Boston is a college city** — 50+ universities, vibrant Indian food scene, cultural activities
- **Weather warning:** Boston winters are harsh (November-March), regularly below -10 C
- **Living groups** — choose from dorms, fraternities/sororities, or independent living groups
