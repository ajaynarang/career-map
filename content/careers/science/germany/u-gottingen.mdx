## Why Gottingen?

Gottingen is mathematics. That sentence is not hyperbole — from the 18th to mid-20th century, Gottingen was simply the most important place in the world for mathematics and theoretical physics. Carl Friedrich Gauss, Bernhard Riemann, David Hilbert, Emmy Noether, Max Born, and Werner Heisenberg all worked here. The Gottingen Mathematics Institute was where modern mathematics was born — group theory, Riemannian geometry, quantum mechanics, and algebraic number theory all trace their origins to this small German university town.

Today, Gottingen continues this legacy through strong mathematics, physics, and chemistry departments, plus deep integration with five Max Planck Institutes located in the city. While LMU Munich and Heidelberg may rank higher in overall university rankings, Gottingen's mathematics and physics programs carry a historical weight and ongoing research quality that few institutions can match.

**The cost advantage:** Gottingen is in Lower Saxony, which charges **no tuition** to any students (including non-EU). The semester fee is about €400 (includes a semester ticket for public transport across the entire state). Living costs are among the lowest of any German university city — ₹9-11L/year total. A 3-year Bachelor's costs about ₹30-35L total. This is extraordinary value for world-class science education.

## How to Get In

Standard German university admission process, but generally less competitive than LMU Munich or Heidelberg:

1. **APS Certificate** from the German Embassy in New Delhi
2. **German language proof:** TestDaF 4x4 or DSH-2
3. **Apply through [uni-assist](https://www.uni-assist.de)** or Gottingen's portal
4. **Studienkolleg** in Gottingen if needed (one of Germany's well-regarded Studienkollegs)

**Application deadlines:**
- Winter semester (October): July 15
- Summer semester (April): January 15

**Good news:** Most science programs at Gottingen do not have a strict NC (Numerus Clausus), making admission more accessible than Munich-based universities. If your child meets the qualification and language requirements, they will likely get a place.

## Curriculum & Structure

Bachelor of Science (BSc) — 3 years (6 semesters).

- **Mathematics:** Gottingen's math curriculum is deeply rigorous and historically rooted. Analysis, algebra, geometry, and number theory are taught in the tradition of Gauss, Riemann, and Hilbert. The department is small but the quality per faculty member is outstanding
- **Physics:** Experimental and theoretical physics with a strong emphasis on mathematical foundations. Specializations in astrophysics, biophysics, condensed matter, and geophysics. The Physics Department has produced multiple Nobel laureates
- **Chemistry:** Strong in physical chemistry (a tradition going back to Walther Nernst) and materials chemistry. Modern facilities and good research funding
- **Interdisciplinary options:** "Physics of Complex Systems" and "Molecular Ecosystem Sciences" (English-taught) are unique interdisciplinary programs
- **GAUSS (Gottingen Graduate Center for Neurosciences, Biophysics, and Molecular Biosciences):** Undergraduates who perform well can access graduate-level seminars and research opportunities through GAUSS

## Research & Labs

Five Max Planck Institutes in Gottingen make it a research powerhouse disproportionate to the city's size:

- **Max Planck Institute for Dynamics and Self-Organization** — fluid dynamics, nonlinear dynamics, biophysics
- **Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences** — molecular biology, neuroscience, chemistry (one of the largest Max Planck Institutes)
- **Max Planck Institute for Solar System Research** — planetary science, solar physics. Contributed instruments to ESA and NASA missions
- **Max Planck Institute for the History of Science** (Berlin-based but collaborates closely with Gottingen)
- **Institute for Astrophysics and Geophysics** — stellar physics, geophysics, strong observational astronomy program
- **XFEL (European X-Ray Free-Electron Laser)** — Hamburg-based but with Gottingen involvement in data analysis and experiments

## Campus Life

Gottingen is the definition of a German university town — small (120,000 people, 30,000 of whom are students), safe, affordable, and entirely centered around the university.

- **"City of Science"** — officially branded as such. The entire city revolves around the university and its research institutes
- **Very affordable** — rent is €300-450/month for a shared apartment. This is 30-40% cheaper than Munich. Mensa meals for €3-4
- **Compact and bikeable** — everything is within cycling distance. Most students don't need public transport
- **Student-friendly atmosphere** — cafes, bookshops, student bars, and a lively student scene relative to the city's size
- **Indian community** — smaller than in Munich or Berlin, but Indian Student Association exists. Frankfurt (2.5 hours by train) has a large Indian community
- **Gauss statue** — the famous mathematician's statue stands in front of the observatory. Walking past it daily is a reminder of the mathematical heritage
- **Safety** — Gottingen is one of the safest cities in Germany. Virtually no serious crime
- **Semester ticket** — your semester fee includes free public transport across all of Lower Saxony (including trains to Hanover, Brunswick, and beyond)
