## Why Caltech for Engineering?

Caltech is the smallest elite research university in the world — only **980 undergraduates** — and arguably the most intense. With 46 Nobel laureates, management of NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL), and a 3:1 student-to-faculty ratio, Caltech delivers an undergraduate engineering experience that is impossible to replicate anywhere else on Earth.

What sets Caltech engineering apart: **every student does research**. The SURF (Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowships) program places undergraduates in active research labs, often producing publishable papers. Caltech's GALCIT (Graduate Aerospace Laboratories) is where modern aerospace engineering was born — founded by Theodore von Karman, mentor of Qian Xuesen and Frank Marble. The legacy runs through every corridor.

**For Indian families, the financial story is remarkable.** Caltech is need-blind for international students and meets 100% of demonstrated financial need. Families earning under $100,000/year pay zero tuition. This can make Caltech cheaper than many Indian private engineering colleges.

## How to Get In

Caltech's **2.5% acceptance rate** is the lowest in the United States — even more selective than MIT, Harvard, or Stanford. But Caltech is looking for a very specific type of student.

**What Caltech wants:**
- **Deep, genuine love for science and engineering** — not career-driven ambition, but intellectual obsession
- **Extraordinary quantitative ability** — virtually every admitted student has competition-level math skills
- **Research or building experience** — published papers, robotics projects, or meaningful engineering work
- **Collaborative mindset** — Caltech's Honor Code system requires trust and teamwork

**Profile of admitted Indian students:** Nearly all have International Olympiad medals (IPhO, IMO, IOI) or equivalent achievements. Many have built significant engineering projects or published research. Perfect scores are the floor, not the ceiling.

**Timeline:**
- Early Action: November 1 (non-binding)
- Regular Decision: January 3
- Decisions: Mid-March

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year Bachelor of Science. Caltech's engineering curriculum is famously rigorous.

- **Core curriculum:** ALL students — including engineers — take 5 terms of math, 5 terms of physics, 2 terms of chemistry, and 1 term of biology. A Caltech engineer takes more pure science than most physics majors elsewhere
- **GALCIT (Aerospace):** One of the world's premier aerospace programs. Wind tunnels, propulsion labs, and close ties with SpaceX (founded by Caltech-adjacent Elon Musk, with many Caltech alumni)
- **Electrical Engineering:** Circuits, signal processing, VLSI, and a strong focus on quantum devices and photonics
- **Mechanical Engineering:** Design, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, with access to world-class machine shops and fabrication labs
- **Small classes:** Average class size is 15-20 students. Professors know you by name
- **Pass/fail first term:** Eases the brutal transition to Caltech's workload
- **SURF:** 10-week paid summer research. Nearly all students participate, many at JPL

## Research & Labs

- **NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)** — managed by Caltech. Mars rovers, Europa missions, deep space networks. Undergraduates can intern and do research at JPL
- **GALCIT** — hypersonic wind tunnels, space structures, and propulsion research
- **Resnick Sustainability Institute** — clean energy, water, and environmental engineering
- **Kavli Nanoscience Institute** — nanotechnology, quantum devices, advanced fabrication
- **LIGO** — gravitational wave detection (2017 Nobel Prize). Caltech co-leads this project

## Campus Life

- **Tiny community:** 980 undergrads means everyone knows each other. It's more like a research monastery than a typical university
- **House system:** 8 student houses, each with distinct personality and traditions. Assigned during Rotation Week
- **Ditch Day** — seniors design elaborate puzzle challenges ("stacks") for underclassmen. Legendary tradition
- **Pasadena/LA:** Year-round sunshine, 20 minutes from downtown LA, close to beaches and mountains. Large Indian diaspora in the area
- **Indian community is small but tight-knit** — about 30-40 Indian undergrads. The broader South Asian community in LA is enormous
- **The workload is real:** 20-30 hours per week on problem sets alone. But students who thrive here wouldn't want it any other way
