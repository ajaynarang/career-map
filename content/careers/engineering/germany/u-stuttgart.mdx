## Why University of Stuttgart?

If your child is interested in **automotive engineering**, there is no better place in the world than the University of Stuttgart. Stuttgart is the home of Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, and Bosch — the holy trinity of German automotive industry. The University of Stuttgart has direct pipelines into these companies for internships, thesis projects, and jobs.

Stuttgart also has Germany's best Aerospace Engineering program and a unique Automotive Engineering (Fahrzeug- und Motorentechnik) major that you won't find at most other universities. For students passionate about cars, planes, or mechatronics, Stuttgart is paradise.

## How to Get In

Standard German admissions process. Like KIT, the University of Stuttgart charges **€1,500/semester** to international students (Baden-Württemberg state fee).

1. **APS Certificate** required
2. **German B2/C1** — all Bachelor's programs are taught in German
3. Apply via uni-assist or the university's own portal
4. Studienkolleg may be required

**Deadlines:** Winter semester: July 15 | Summer semester: January 15

## Curriculum & Structure

Bachelor's: 3 years (6 semesters), typically 7-8 in practice.

- **Fahrzeug- und Motorentechnik** is unique — a dedicated automotive engineering degree covering vehicle dynamics, engine design, and autonomous driving
- **Strong industry thesis partnerships** — most students write their thesis at Daimler, Porsche, or Bosch
- **Mandatory internships** — typically at companies in the Stuttgart region
- **Interdisciplinary projects** — Stuttgart emphasizes combining engineering disciplines (mechanical + electrical + software)
- **Excellent aerospace labs** — wind tunnels, flight simulators, and partnerships with DLR (German Aerospace Center)

## Campus Life

Stuttgart is a wealthy industrial city set in a valley surrounded by vineyards. It's not as hip as Berlin, but it's prosperous and well-organized.

- **Highest engineering salaries in Germany** — Baden-Württemberg pays the best. Graduates often stay in the region
- **Moderate cost of living** — rent is €400-550/month for a shared flat. More affordable than Munich, pricier than eastern Germany
- **Indian community** — decent sized, with Indian restaurants and cultural events
- **Excellent public transport** — Stuttgart's S-Bahn and U-Bahn system is efficient. Semester ticket covers it
- **Proximity to the Black Forest** — hiking, skiing, and nature on weekends
- **Conservative culture** — Stuttgart is more traditional than Berlin. The Swabian culture values hard work and thrift (Schaffe, schaffe, Häusle baue)
