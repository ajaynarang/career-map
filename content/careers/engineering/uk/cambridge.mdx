## Why Cambridge?

Cambridge's Engineering department is unique in the UK — and arguably the world. Rather than forcing students to choose a narrow specialization at age 18, Cambridge teaches **broad-based engineering** for the first two years. Your child studies electrical, mechanical, civil, and information engineering together before specializing in Year 3. This produces engineers who can think across disciplines — a skill that's increasingly valuable.

Cambridge is also the birthplace of ARM (the chip architecture in every smartphone), Raspberry Pi, and dozens of deep-tech startups. The "Silicon Fen" tech cluster around Cambridge is the UK's answer to Silicon Valley.

## How to Get In

Cambridge's application process is more involved than most UK universities:

1. **UCAS application** by **October 15** (early deadline)
2. **ESAT (Engineering and Science Admissions Test)** — a standardized aptitude test introduced in 2025 replacing the ENGAA. Tests mathematical and scientific reasoning
3. **College selection** — Cambridge is a collegiate university. You apply to a specific college (or make an open application). Each college has its own character
4. **Interview** — Cambridge interviews all shortlisted candidates. For international students, interviews are conducted online (or at specific centers in India). This is critical — strong academic thinking under pressure is tested
5. **Conditional offer** — typically A*A*A at A-Level or equivalent (95%+ in Indian boards)

**Reality check for Indian students:** Cambridge engineering receives ~2,000 applications for ~300 places. The interview is the key differentiator — your child must demonstrate ability to think through unfamiliar problems in real-time.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year BA + MEng. Cambridge's engineering course is uniquely structured:

- **Years 1-2 (Parts IA & IB):** All students take the same broad engineering curriculum — math, structures, mechanics, electronics, thermodynamics, computing, business
- **Year 3 (Part IIA):** Choose a specialization area (Electrical, Mechanical, Civil, Aerospace, Information & Computer, etc.)
- **Year 4 (Part IIB):** Advanced specialization + individual research project
- **Supervisions** — small-group teaching (1-3 students with an expert) unique to Cambridge and Oxford. This is where deep learning happens
- **Tripos exam system** — end-of-year exams determine your class (First, 2:1, 2:2). Continuous assessment is minimal
- **Industrial experience** — encouraged between Years 2-3 or 3-4, though not mandatory

## Campus Life

Cambridge is a beautiful, historic city of 145,000 people, dominated by the university. The college system defines the experience.

- **College life is central** — your child will eat, sleep, socialize, and receive supervisions within their college. Each college has its own dining hall, library, bar, and traditions
- **Indian community is strong** — Cambridge University Indian Society (CUIS) is very active. Major Diwali and Holi celebrations
- **Punting on the Cam** — the iconic Cambridge experience
- **Formal Halls** — college dinners in historic halls wearing academic gowns. A unique tradition
- **Cambridge is small** — you can cycle everywhere. Most students bike. It's safe and walkable
- **Academically intense** — Cambridge terms are 8 weeks (vs. 10-12 at most universities). The pace is fast and demanding
- **London is 50 minutes by train** — access to London's job market and social scene on weekends
- **May Balls** — legendary end-of-year parties held by each college (confusingly held in June)
