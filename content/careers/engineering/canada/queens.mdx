## Why Queen's University?

Queen's is the "Oxford of Canada" — a prestigious university known for its tight-knit community, strong alumni network, and excellent student experience. While it may not have the raw research power of U of T or Waterloo, Queen's engineering graduates are disproportionately well-represented in leadership positions across Canadian industry, mining, and finance.

Queen's Mining Engineering is consistently #1-2 in Canada — the mining and geological engineering programs have direct pipelines into major mining companies worldwide. The engineering program also has one of the strongest placement records in Canada, with graduates highly sought after for their combination of technical skills and leadership ability.

## How to Get In

Queen's accepts **30-40%** of international engineering applicants.

**Application:**
- Apply through **OUAC** (105F for international students)
- **PSE (Personal Statement of Experience)** required — essays about leadership, community involvement, and personal qualities
- Deadline: **February 1** (OUAC + PSE)
- 87-92%+ in Indian boards

Queen's values well-rounded applicants — the PSE is an important differentiator.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year Bachelor of Applied Science (BASc). Semester system.

- **First year is common** — all engineering students take core courses before choosing a discipline at the end of Year 1
- **16-month internship program** — between Years 3 and 4, similar to U of T's PEY. Paid work at companies like Hatch, Glencore, P&G
- **Engineering & Commerce option** — Queen's offers a unique dual degree allowing engineering students to also earn a commerce degree in 5 years
- **Strong design emphasis** — Queen's engineering capstone projects are known for their quality and industry relevance
- **Mining and Geological Engineering** — world-class programs with field camps and industry partnerships
- **Complementary Studies** — Queen's Engineering includes required courses in engineering ethics, economics, and communication

## Campus Life

Queen's campus overlooks Lake Ontario in Kingston, a small, historic city (130,000 people) between Toronto and Montreal.

- **"Queen's Spirit"** — the school spirit at Queen's is legendary. Students are deeply loyal to the university. Homecoming is massive
- **Small-town charm** — Kingston is walkable, safe, and beautiful. The campus and downtown are integrated
- **Indian community** — smaller than major cities but present. Queen's Indian Association organizes cultural events. Indian restaurants available in Kingston
- **Tight-knit community** — Queen's is smaller than U of T or UBC, which means closer relationships with professors and peers
- **Cold winters** — Kingston gets cold (-15°C to -25°C) with significant snowfall and Lake Ontario winds
- **Affordable** — rent is CAD 600-900/month. Kingston is cheaper than Toronto or Vancouver
- **Between Toronto and Montreal** — Kingston is 2.5 hours from Toronto and 2.5 hours from Montreal by car or Via Rail train
