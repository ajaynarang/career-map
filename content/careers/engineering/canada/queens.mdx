## Why Queen's?

Queen's University is one of Canada's oldest and most respected universities, known for its exceptionally tight-knit community, strong alumni network, and excellent engineering programs. The Smith School of Engineering is smaller than U of T or Waterloo, and that's a feature, not a bug — class sizes are manageable, professors know students by name, and the collaborative culture produces confident, well-rounded engineers.

Queen's engineering graduates are highly sought after in mining, infrastructure, finance, and tech. The alumni network (called the "Queen's network") is legendarily strong — Queen's grads help each other professionally throughout their careers. For Indian families who value community, mentorship, and a supportive learning environment alongside academic rigor, Queen's is an excellent choice.

## How to Get In

Queen's Engineering accepts **30-40%** of international applicants — competitive but not as cutthroat as Waterloo.

**Application process:**
- Apply through **OUAC** (Ontario Universities' Application Centre)
- Complete the **PSE (Personal Statement of Experience)** — short essays about leadership, teamwork, and interest in engineering
- Deadline: **February 1** for Fall admission
- IELTS 6.5+ or TOEFL 88+
- 87%+ in Indian boards (Math, Physics, Chemistry)

**Queen's values community fit.** The PSE is important — they want students who will contribute to the collaborative Queen's culture, not just study in isolation.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year BASc or 5-year BASc + internship option.

- **Year 1:** Common first-year engineering — calculus, physics, chemistry, programming, design, and professional skills
- **Year 2 onward:** Choose specialization. Queen's allows more flexibility than some schools — you can switch disciplines more easily in early years
- **12-16 month internship** — optional paid internship between Years 3 and 4, extending the degree to 5 years. Many students intern at mining companies, banks, or tech firms
- **Mining Engineering** — Queen's is historically one of Canada's top mining schools. With global demand for critical minerals, mining engineering graduates have excellent job prospects
- **Engineering Physics** — a rigorous, theory-heavy program that feeds into graduate school and research careers
- **Integrated Learning Centre (ILC)** — Queen's Engineering's state-of-the-art facility with advanced labs, maker spaces, and collaborative work areas
- **TEAM projects** — team-based design challenges integrated throughout the curriculum
- **Professional development** — Queen's emphasizes communication, ethics, and leadership alongside technical skills

## Campus Life

Kingston is a charming city of 135,000 on Lake Ontario, halfway between Toronto and Montreal. It's a classic university town where the university IS the city.

- **True university town** — Kingston revolves around Queen's. The campus and downtown are integrated. Walk everywhere
- **Beautiful setting** — on the shores of Lake Ontario. The Thousand Islands are nearby. Gorgeous in fall and summer
- **Indian community is smaller but close-knit** — Kingston's Indian community is smaller than Toronto's but the university's Indian Students' Association is active and welcoming
- **Very affordable** — rent is CAD 500-700/month. Kingston is significantly cheaper than Toronto or Vancouver
- **Queen's spirit is legendary** — Homecoming, Orientation Week, and the engineering tradition of "Grease Pole" climbing are iconic. Queen's students have the strongest school pride in Canada
- **Safe and walkable** — Kingston is one of the safest cities in Canada. Everything is within walking distance
- **Cold winters** — Kingston gets cold (-15°C to -20°C) with lake-effect weather. Proper winter gear is essential
- **Toronto and Montreal accessible** — both are 2.5-3 hours by car/bus, easy for weekend trips
