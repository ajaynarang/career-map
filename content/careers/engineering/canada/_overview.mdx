## Why Canada for Engineering?

Canada is increasingly the top choice for Indian students — and for good reason. It combines strong engineering programs (University of Toronto and Waterloo are globally ranked), affordable costs compared to the US and UK, and the clearest immigration pathway of any English-speaking country. After graduating, your child gets a **3-year open work permit** (PGWP), and Canada actively encourages skilled graduates to apply for **Permanent Residency (PR)** through Express Entry.

**The financial picture:** A 4-year engineering degree in Canada costs ₹1.2-1.8 Crore total — significantly less than the US (₹2-3 Crore) or UK (₹1.7-2.6 Crore). Living costs outside Toronto and Vancouver are very reasonable by Western standards.

## The Path

1. **Class 11-12:** Focus on board exams (Math, Physics, Chemistry). Prepare for IELTS (aim for 6.5-7.0)
2. **Summer before 12th:** Research universities. Each Canadian university has its own application portal (no common app like the US)
3. **October-January of 12th:** Apply directly to universities — most deadlines are January-February for Fall admission
4. **February-May:** Conditional offers arrive based on predicted/actual grades
5. **After 12th results (June-July):** Meet conditions, accept offer, apply for Study Permit
6. **September:** Start university

## What Parents Should Know

- **No SAT/ACT required.** Most Canadian universities admit based on 12th board results (typically 85-93%+ for top engineering programs). This makes applications simpler than the US.
- **Co-op is king.** Canadian engineering programs (especially Waterloo) have exceptional co-operative education programs where students alternate between study and paid work terms. Your child can earn CAD 50,000-80,000+ over their co-op terms, offsetting tuition costs.
- **PGWP + Express Entry = PR pathway.** After a 4-year degree, your child gets a 3-year open work permit. After 1 year of skilled work, they can apply for Permanent Residency through Express Entry. Canada actively wants skilled engineers to stay.
- **Each university has its own portal.** Unlike the US (Common App) or UK (UCAS), Canadian universities require separate applications. Apply to 4-6 universities directly.
- **French in Quebec.** McGill University in Montreal is English-medium, but daily life in Montreal involves some French. This is an enriching experience, not a barrier.
- **Harsh winters.** Canadian winters are genuinely cold (-20°C to -30°C in many cities). Your child will adapt, but budget for proper winter clothing.
- **Provincial healthcare.** Some provinces (Ontario, British Columbia) provide free healthcare to international students via provincial health plans. Others require private insurance.
- **Professional engineering licensing.** In Canada, "Engineer" is a protected title. Graduates must work under a licensed P.Eng. for 4 years, then pass an ethics exam to earn the P.Eng. designation. The degree itself is accredited by Engineers Canada.
