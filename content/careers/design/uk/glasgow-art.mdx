## Why Glasgow School of Art?

The Glasgow School of Art (GSA) is one of Europe's oldest and most prestigious independent art schools, founded in 1845. It is the school of Charles Rennie Mackintosh — Scotland's most celebrated architect and designer, whose Art Nouveau masterwork, the Mackintosh Building, served as GSA's iconic home for over a century. GSA's Product Design program is internationally recognized for its emphasis on innovation, user-centered thinking, and the integration of design with engineering.

**For Indian families:** GSA offers an exceptional combination that is hard to find elsewhere:

1. **Significantly lower costs than London schools.** Tuition is £19,440/year (compared to £35,000+ at RCA or CSM), and Glasgow's living costs are 40-50% lower than London. A 4-year BDes at GSA costs approximately ₹1.3 Crore total — compared to ₹2+ Crore at London alternatives.

2. **Scotland's 4-year degree system.** Scottish universities offer 4-year Honours degrees (vs. 3 years in England). This means an extra year of studio practice and deeper skill development. Indian students entering with Class 12 qualifications benefit from this additional year.

3. **Product Design Engineering (joint with University of Glasgow).** GSA's unique MSc in Product Design Engineering is delivered jointly with the University of Glasgow — a top 100 global university. This program bridges design and engineering, ideal for PCM students who want both creative and technical skills. Graduates are in high demand at firms like Dyson, Apple, and automotive companies.

**The innovation culture:** GSA emphasizes design for real-world impact. Student projects frequently involve partnerships with NHS Scotland (healthcare design), social enterprises, and technology companies. Graduates don't just make beautiful objects — they solve problems.

## How to Get In

GSA's admission is portfolio-driven:

1. **UCAS application:** Apply by January 31 through UCAS. Personal statement should demonstrate genuine passion for design, making, and problem-solving.
2. **Portfolio (15-25 pieces):** Submit a digital portfolio showing design projects, sketches, model photographs, material experiments, and observational drawing. GSA values the design process — include rough sketches, iterations, and evidence of how ideas evolve.
3. **Interview:** Shortlisted candidates are invited for a portfolio interview (available online for international students). Be prepared to discuss your work in depth — why you made certain decisions, what you learned, and how you would improve.
4. **Academic requirements:** BBB at A-Level or equivalent. Indian students: 70%+ in Class 12 boards.
5. **IELTS:** 6.0 overall with minimum 5.5 in each component.

**For Indian applicants:** GSA is looking for curiosity, making skills, and design thinking — not formal art school training. If your child builds things, takes apart objects to understand them, sketches ideas, or has a passion for understanding how products work, they are a strong GSA candidate. Document these activities in the portfolio.

**Scotland's Saltire Scholarships:** Available specifically for Indian nationals — £8,000 toward tuition. Apply early as these are competitive.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year BDes (Hons) Product Design:

- **Year 1 (Foundation):** Introduction to design methods, drawing, model-making, materials, digital tools, and design history. Students learn to sketch, prototype, and communicate design ideas
- **Year 2 (Development):** User-centered design, design research methods, manufacturing processes, CAD (SolidWorks, Rhino), sustainability in design. Studio projects increase in complexity — from simple objects to systems
- **Year 3 (Specialization):** Advanced studio projects, often with industry partners or live briefs. Students develop expertise in specific areas: consumer products, medical devices, furniture, interactive products, or sustainable design. Many students undertake an exchange semester at a partner school (Pratt, RISD, or European institutions)
- **Year 4 (Honours):** Degree project — a substantial, self-directed design project exhibited at the GSA Degree Show. The Degree Show is a major event attended by employers, press, and the design community. Strong projects lead directly to job offers and media coverage

**MSc Product Design Engineering (joint with University of Glasgow):**
A 1-year postgraduate program combining GSA's design expertise with Glasgow University's engineering facilities. Students develop products from concept to engineered prototype. This is an exceptional option for Indian B.Tech graduates who want to add design skills to their engineering foundation.

**Workshop facilities:**
- Wood workshop, metal workshop, plastics workshop
- Digital fabrication: laser cutters, CNC routers, 3D printers (FDM, SLA)
- Ceramics and casting facilities
- Electronics prototyping lab
- Photography and video studios

## Campus & City Life

GSA's campus is in the heart of Glasgow's city center:

- **The Reid Building** (designed by Steven Holl Architects) is GSA's main teaching building — a striking contemporary structure with generous studio spaces, workshops, and exhibition areas
- **The Mackintosh Building:** Charles Rennie Mackintosh's masterpiece was severely damaged by fires in 2014 and 2018. A major restoration is underway. The building remains central to GSA's identity and history
- **Glasgow is Scotland's largest city** — a vibrant, multicultural, student-friendly city with world-class museums (Kelvingrove, Riverside Museum, Gallery of Modern Art), a thriving music scene, and excellent food
- **Cost of living is low for the UK.** Rent for a shared flat: £350-500/month. Groceries, transport, and entertainment are significantly cheaper than London. Many students find Glasgow surprisingly affordable
- **Indian community:** Glasgow has a substantial South Asian community, particularly in the Southside (Pollokshields). Indian grocery stores, restaurants, and temples are easily accessible. The University of Glasgow has an active Indian Students' Society
- **Design industry in Glasgow:** Scotland has a growing design sector. Firms like 4c Design, Timorous Beasties, and NCR Atleos are Glasgow-based. Edinburgh (1 hour by train) adds additional opportunities
- **Student city:** Glasgow has over 130,000 students across its universities. The city's culture revolves around its student population, making it welcoming and affordable for young people
