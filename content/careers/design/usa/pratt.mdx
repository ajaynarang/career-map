## Why Pratt Institute?

Pratt Institute is one of America's most respected design schools, consistently ranked in the global top 10 for art and design. Founded in 1887 in Brooklyn, Pratt has a 135-year legacy of producing designers who shape industries — from the industrial designers behind iconic consumer products to the communication designers who define visual culture. Pratt's Industrial Design program is STEM-designated, which is a critical advantage for international students seeking post-graduation work rights in the US.

**For Indian families:** Pratt offers three key advantages over its competitors (RISD, Parsons):

1. **Higher acceptance rate with generous merit scholarships.** While RISD admits ~16% of applicants, Pratt admits ~42%. And Pratt's merit scholarships (up to full tuition) are portfolio-based — a talented Indian student with a strong portfolio can receive $15,000-$30,000/year in scholarships, making Pratt significantly more affordable than RISD or Parsons.

2. **STEM designation for Industrial Design.** This means graduates get 3 years of OPT (work permission) in the US after graduation, compared to 1 year for non-STEM programs. Three years to work, earn, and build a career in the US before needing H-1B sponsorship.

3. **Brooklyn location.** Pratt's campus is in Clinton Hill, Brooklyn — one of New York's most culturally vibrant neighborhoods. Students are immersed in NYC's design industry. Every major design firm, agency, museum, and gallery is accessible by subway. NYC internships and part-time jobs are plentiful.

**The portfolio is everything:** Pratt's admissions are portfolio-driven. A strong portfolio from an Indian student with no formal art school background can absolutely earn admission and significant scholarships. This is a meritocracy.

## How to Get In

Pratt's admissions process is portfolio-centered:

1. **Portfolio (12-20 pieces):** Show a range of creative work — drawing, painting, photography, digital design, sculpture, product prototypes, whatever your child creates. Include work that demonstrates observation, creativity, and problem-solving.
2. **Visual Essay:** 3-5 observational drawings from direct observation (not from photographs). Pratt wants to see how your child sees the world. Still life, figure drawing, urban sketching, and environmental studies are all appropriate.
3. **Common Application** + Pratt supplement. Essay should articulate design interests and creative philosophy.
4. **SAT/ACT:** Optional for most programs.
5. **TOEFL 79+ or IELTS 6.5+** for international students.
6. **Recommendation letters:** One teacher recommendation required.

**Application timeline:**
- Early Action: November 1 (non-binding — recommended for best scholarship consideration)
- Regular Decision: January 5
- Decisions: December (EA), late March (RD)

**Portfolio strategy for Indian students:** If your child has not attended formal art classes, that is completely fine. Pratt values raw talent and authentic vision. Include sketchbook pages showing daily observations, photographs of interesting textures/patterns/spaces, and any design experiments (even amateur ones). Process and curiosity matter more than polish.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year BFA (Bachelor of Fine Arts). All students take a common Foundation Year:

- **Year 1 (Foundation):** Drawing, 2D Design, 3D Design, Light/Color/Design, and art history. Every student, regardless of major, goes through this intensive visual education. The Foundation year is rigorous — students learn to see, think, and communicate visually
- **Years 2-3 (Major Coursework):** Studio courses in chosen major + liberal arts and sciences (approximately 30% of credits). Students develop specialized skills while maintaining broad intellectual growth

**Industrial Design (BFA):**
- Product design methodology, user research, ergonomics, materials and manufacturing, CAD (SolidWorks, Rhino), prototyping, design for sustainability
- Industry-sponsored projects with companies like Samsung, OXO, and Method
- Senior thesis: a comprehensive design project exhibited at the year-end Senior Show
- **STEM-designated** — 3 years of OPT work authorization after graduation

**Communications Design (BFA):**
- Typography, branding, editorial design, motion graphics, UX/UI design, packaging design, advertising
- Students choose between Graphic Design, Illustration, and Advertising tracks
- Industry projects and internships with NYC agencies and design firms

## Campus Life

Pratt's 25-acre campus in Clinton Hill, Brooklyn, is one of the most beautiful urban campuses in the US:

- **A green oasis in Brooklyn** — Pratt's campus features historic buildings, sculpture gardens, and tree-lined paths. The campus is a designated landmark with a peaceful atmosphere despite being in the heart of NYC
- **Fabrication facilities** are extensive — CNC routers, laser cutters, 3D printers (FDM, SLA, SLS), wood shop, metal shop, ceramics studio, and digital media labs. Industrial Design students have dedicated workshop access
- **Brooklyn location** — Clinton Hill and neighboring Fort Greene are vibrant, diverse neighborhoods with cafes, galleries, and a thriving creative community. Manhattan is 15 minutes by subway
- **NYC as a design classroom** — MoMA, the Cooper Hewitt, the Met, the Brooklyn Museum, design firms (Pentagram, Smart Design, IDEO NYC), tech companies, and fashion houses are all accessible. Students regularly visit exhibitions and attend industry events
- **Indian community** — NYC has one of the largest Indian populations in the US. Jackson Heights (Queens) is a 30-minute subway ride and offers extensive Indian restaurants, grocery stores, and cultural events
- **Cost of living in Brooklyn** is high but manageable for students. On-campus housing: $12,000-16,000/year. Meal plan: $5,000-6,000/year. Many students move off-campus after freshman year for lower costs
