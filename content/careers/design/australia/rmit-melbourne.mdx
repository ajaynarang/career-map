## Why RMIT Melbourne?

RMIT (Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology) is Australia's #1 design school and ranks #11 globally for Art & Design — ahead of many prestigious European and American institutions. Founded in 1887, RMIT has a long tradition of practice-based education: students learn by doing, making, and building. Its Melbourne CBD campus is surrounded by the city's legendary creative scene — street art, independent galleries, design studios, and a thriving coffee culture.

**For Indian families:** RMIT offers a globally respected design degree at a total cost of approximately ₹1.3 Crore for 4 years — competitive with Canadian options and far below US/UK alternatives. Melbourne's design industry is growing, with UX designers and product designers in strong demand at tech companies (Atlassian, Canva, REA Group) and consultancies.

**The practical advantage:** RMIT's design programs are deeply connected to industry. Students complete internships, work on live industry briefs, and build portfolios that employers recognize. Graduates don't just have a degree — they have a body of work and professional connections.

## How to Get In

RMIT admissions for design combine academic results and portfolio review.

1. **Online Application:** Apply directly through RMIT's international student portal. No centralized system required.
2. **Portfolio (10-15 pieces):** Submit digitally. Show creative range — drawing, digital design, photography, 3D work. RMIT wants to see curiosity, experimentation, and creative potential. Process documentation is valued.
3. **Academic transcripts:** Indian Class 12 boards accepted. Minimum 65% overall; competitive applicants have 70%+.
4. **IELTS:** 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0.

**Application timeline:**
- Semester 1 (February start): Apply by October-November of previous year
- Semester 2 (July start): Apply by April-May
- Offers are rolling — apply early for best scholarship chances

**Pathway option:** If your child's academic results don't meet direct entry requirements, RMIT offers Foundation Studies and Diploma pathways that lead into the second year of the Bachelor's degree.

## Curriculum & Structure

4-year Bachelor of Design. RMIT's programs are studio-based — students spend significant time in design studios, workshops, and labs.

**Industrial Design:**
- Product design, furniture, consumer electronics, sustainable design, design for manufacturing
- Access to wood/metal workshops, CNC machines, 3D printers, laser cutters, and prototyping labs
- Industry placement in Year 3 (paid internship at a design company)

**Communication Design:**
- Graphic design, branding, UI/UX design, motion graphics, editorial design, packaging
- Strong digital skills alongside traditional design craft
- Industry projects with real clients throughout the degree

**Digital Media:**
- Interactive design, creative coding, game design, immersive media (VR/AR)
- Ideal for PCM students who want to combine design with technology
- Growing industry demand in gaming, tech, and digital experience design

**All programs include:**
- **Industry placement** — a mandatory work placement (typically 3-6 months) in Year 3 or 4
- **Capstone project** — a major final-year project exhibited at RMIT's graduate exhibition
- **Electives** — students can take courses across RMIT's faculties (engineering, business, science)

## Campus & City Life

RMIT's main campus is in the heart of Melbourne's CBD — unlike suburban universities, RMIT is embedded in the city.

- **Melbourne's creative culture** — RMIT students are surrounded by galleries, design studios, street art (Hosier Lane is steps from campus), independent fashion, and Australia's best food scene
- **State-of-the-art facilities** — design studios, digital fabrication labs, media labs, and the RMIT Design Hub (a striking building that houses exhibitions and research)
- **Large Indian student community** — RMIT has one of the largest Indian student populations in Australia. Melbourne has a massive Indian community with temples, grocery stores, restaurants, and cultural events
- **Affordable by Australian standards** — Melbourne is cheaper than Sydney. Shared apartments: AUD 200-280/week in inner suburbs. The free tram zone covers the CBD and campus area
- **Part-time work** — Melbourne's hospitality, retail, and creative industries offer abundant part-time work for students. At AUD 24+/hour minimum wage, 20 hours/week provides meaningful income
