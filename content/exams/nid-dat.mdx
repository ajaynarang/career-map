## What is NID DAT?

NID DAT (Design Aptitude Test) is the entrance exam for the **National Institute of Design (NID)** — India's oldest and most famous design school, established in 1961 in Ahmedabad. NID has 4 campuses: Ahmedabad (main), Gandhinagar, Kurukshetra, and Jorhat.

NID offers a **4-year Bachelor of Design (B.Des)** across multiple disciplines:
- Industrial Design
- Communication Design (Graphic Design)
- Textile & Apparel Design
- Ceramic & Glass Design
- Film & Video Communication
- Animation Film Design
- Exhibition Design

**NID vs UCEED:** UCEED gets you into IIT design programmes (more analytical, product/UX focused). NID is more artistically oriented — if your child loves hands-on making, crafts, materials, and visual storytelling, NID is the better choice.

## Scoring & What It Means

NID DAT has two stages:

| Stage | What happens |
|-------|-------------|
| **Prelims** | Written test — MCQ + drawing + design questions. Top 600-700 students qualify for Studio Test |
| **Studio Test (Mains)** | Full-day campus visit — hands-on design tasks, model making, group discussion, personal interview |
| **Final Merit** | Combined score of Prelims + Studio Test. About 100-120 B.Des seats across all campuses |

**Key insight:** The Studio Test is what really matters. Your child needs to demonstrate creative thinking, ability to work with materials (paper, clay, wire), and articulate design ideas clearly.

## How to Prepare

**When to start:** 6-12 months before the Prelims (June-July of the year before).

**Prelims Strategy:**
- **Drawing is essential** — Practice daily. Sketch objects, people, scenes. Develop your own style.
- **Design awareness** — Study Indian crafts, materials, and design history. NID values Indian design sensibility.
- **Visual reasoning** — Pattern recognition, colour perception, spatial thinking
- **Creative thinking** — Practice divergent thinking exercises. NID asks open-ended questions like "Design a toy for a visually impaired child."

**Studio Test Strategy:**
- **Material handling** — Practice working with paper (cutting, folding, scoring), clay, wire, cardboard. NID values hands-on skills.
- **Model making** — Build 3D models of objects from given materials
- **Portfolio** — While not formally required, bring any creative work (sketches, paintings, photographs, handmade objects)
- **Interview** — Be authentic. Talk about why design interests you, what you observe in everyday life, your creative process.

**Resources:**
- Previous year NID DAT papers (available on coaching institute websites)
- "The Design of Everyday Things" by Don Norman
