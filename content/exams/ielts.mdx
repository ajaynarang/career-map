## What is IELTS?

IELTS is the world's most widely accepted English proficiency test, recognized by 11,000+ organizations in 140+ countries. For Indian students, IELTS is required for university admissions in the UK, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and increasingly in the USA and Germany (for English-taught programmes).

**IELTS Academic vs IELTS General Training:**
- **IELTS Academic** — for university admissions. This is what your child needs.
- **IELTS General Training** — for immigration and work visas. Not needed for university applications.

**IELTS vs TOEFL:** IELTS is preferred in the UK, Australia, and Canada. TOEFL is traditionally preferred in the USA (though most US universities now accept both). If applying to multiple countries, IELTS is the safer bet as it's more universally accepted.

**Key advantage of IELTS:** The speaking test is a face-to-face conversation with a real examiner (not a recording like TOEFL). Many Indian students prefer this format as it feels more natural.

## Scoring & What It Means

IELTS uses a band system from 1.0 to 9.0:

| Band | Level | What it gets you |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 8.0-9.0 | Expert/Very Good | Oxford, Cambridge, top UK/Australian universities. Exceeds all requirements. |
| 7.0-7.5 | Good | Meets requirements for most universities worldwide. Standard requirement for top UK/Canadian/Australian universities. |
| 6.5 | Competent | Minimum for many universities. Sufficient for most Australian, Canadian, and some UK universities. |
| 6.0 | Adequate | Foundation/pathway programmes. May get conditional admission with English support. |
| Below 6.0 | Limited | Not accepted by most universities. Consider retaking after more preparation. |

**Typical university requirements:**

| Country | Usual Minimum |
|---------|--------------|
| UK (Russell Group) | 6.5-7.0 overall, no band below 6.0 |
| Australia (Group of Eight) | 6.5-7.0 overall |
| Canada (U15 Group) | 6.5-7.0 overall |
| Germany (English programmes) | 6.0-6.5 overall |
| USA (when IELTS accepted) | 6.5-7.0 overall |

## How to Prepare

**When to start:** 2-3 months before the test if your English is already good (English-medium schooling). 4-6 months if you need significant improvement.

**Section-wise strategy:**

**Listening (30 min):**
- Practice with BBC podcasts, TED Talks, and IELTS listening samples
- Learn to listen for specific information while reading questions simultaneously
- Practice note-taking — you only hear each recording once
- Common trap: spelling errors in fill-in-the-blank answers cost full marks

**Reading (60 min):**
- You have 3 passages with 40 questions in 60 minutes — time management is critical
- Don't read the full passage first — skim, then scan for answers
- Practice with Cambridge IELTS books (the only authentic practice material)
- True/False/Not Given questions are the trickiest — practice these specifically

**Writing (60 min):**
- **Task 1 (150 words, 20 min):** Describe a graph, chart, table, or diagram. Practice interpreting data and writing structured descriptions. Use phrases like "increased significantly," "remained stable," "peaked at."
- **Task 2 (250 words, 40 min):** Write an argumentative essay. Structure: Introduction (paraphrase the question + your position) → Body 1 → Body 2 → Conclusion. Practice writing essays on topics like education, technology, environment, health.
- **Scoring tip:** Vocabulary range and grammatical accuracy matter more than complex ideas. Use varied vocabulary and sentence structures.

**Speaking (11-14 min):**
- Part 1: General questions about yourself (2-3 min) — practice answering common topics (hometown, hobbies, studies)
- Part 2: Cue card — speak for 2 minutes on a given topic. Practice speaking for exactly 2 minutes on random topics (use a timer)
- Part 3: Follow-up discussion — deeper questions on the Part 2 topic. Practice expressing opinions with reasons and examples.
- **Key tip:** Fluency > accuracy. Don't pause to think of the "perfect" word. Keep talking naturally, self-correct if needed, but maintain flow.

**Resources:**
- Cambridge IELTS Books 1-18 (gold standard — use the latest 4-5 books for practice)
- IELTS Liz (YouTube + website) — free, excellent tips
- British Council IELTS preparation course (free online)
- Band 9 sample essays — study the structure and vocabulary
