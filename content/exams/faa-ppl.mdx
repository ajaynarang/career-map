## What is the FAA PPL?

The FAA PPL (Private Pilot License) is the first step to becoming a commercial pilot in the United States. Issued by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), the PPL allows you to fly single-engine aircraft. It's the foundation — after PPL, you progress to Instrument Rating, Commercial Pilot License (CPL), and eventually Airline Transport Pilot License (ATPL).

**Why train in the USA instead of India?**
| Factor | USA | India |
|--------|-----|-------|
| **Total cost to CPL** | $40,000-60,000 (₹35-50 lakh) | ₹40-80 lakh |
| **Training duration** | 12-18 months | 18-24 months |
| **Aircraft availability** | Abundant — rarely wait for aircraft | Limited — long wait times at many FTOs |
| **Weather for flying** | Florida, Arizona, California — flyable 300+ days/year | Monsoon disrupts training 3-4 months/year |
| **Global recognition** | FAA license converted worldwide | DGCA license requires conversion |

**The catch:** To fly commercially in India, you must convert your FAA CPL to a DGCA CPL by passing DGCA ground exams. Many pilots train in the USA (cheaper, faster) and convert back.

## Scoring & What It Means

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Knowledge Test (written)** | 60 MCQ, pass mark 70% (42/60 correct). Valid for 24 months. |
| **Practical Test (checkride)** | Oral exam (1-2 hours) + flight test (1-1.5 hours) with an FAA examiner. Pass/Fail. |
| **Minimum flight hours** | 40 hours total (Part 61) or 35 hours (Part 141). Most students need 60-70 hours to be ready. |

**Part 61 vs Part 141 flight schools:**
- **Part 141:** Structured curriculum, FAA-approved syllabus, faster training, required for international students on M-1 visa. Examples: ATP Flight School, L3Harris.
- **Part 61:** Flexible schedule, good for US residents, often cheaper per hour but takes longer.

## How to Prepare

**Step-by-step process for Indian students:**

1. **Research flight schools** — Look for Part 141 schools in Florida, Arizona, or California (best weather). Check reviews on pilot forums.
2. **Apply for M-1 visa** — Student visa for vocational training. The flight school issues an I-20 form.
3. **TSA clearance** — All international flight students need Transportation Security Administration (TSA) approval. Apply through AFSP (Alien Flight Student Program). Takes 2-4 weeks.
4. **FAA Medical** — Get a Class 3 (minimum) or Class 1 (recommended) medical certificate from an FAA-designated Aviation Medical Examiner (AME) in the USA.
5. **Start training** — Ground school + flight training. PPL takes 2-4 months.
6. **Pass Knowledge Test** — Study using:
   - King Schools or Sporty's online ground school ($250-350)
   - ASA Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (free PDF from FAA)
   - Practice tests on Sporty's or Prepware
7. **Pass Practical Test (checkride)** — your instructor will sign you off when you're ready.

**After PPL, the path continues:**
- Instrument Rating (IFR) — fly in clouds and poor visibility
- Commercial Pilot License (CPL) — fly for money, need 250 total hours
- Multi-Engine Rating — fly twin-engine aircraft
- Certified Flight Instructor (CFI) — teach others to fly (common way to build hours)
- ATPL — airline transport pilot, need 1,500 hours
