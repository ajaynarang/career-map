## What is NATA?

NATA (National Aptitude Test in Architecture) is the primary entrance exam for **B.Arch (Bachelor of Architecture)** admission across India. It's conducted by the Council of Architecture (CoA) and is accepted by most architecture colleges in India except IITs and NITs (which use JEE Main Paper 2).

Architecture is a 5-year degree programme. If your child loves drawing, design, and spatial thinking, NATA is their gateway to becoming an architect.

**NATA vs JEE Main Paper 2:**
- **JEE Main Paper 2** — For admission to IITs (B.Arch at IIT Kharagpur, IIT Roorkee), NITs, and other centrally funded colleges
- **NATA** — For admission to SPA Delhi, SPA Bhopal, CEPT Ahmedabad, and 400+ other architecture colleges
- Many top colleges accept **both** scores. Apply for both.

## Scoring & What It Means

| Score Range | What it gets you |
|------------|-----------------|
| 160+ | SPA Delhi, CEPT Ahmedabad (top architecture schools) |
| 130-160 | Good state-level architecture colleges |
| 100-130 | Mid-tier architecture colleges |
| Below 100 | Lower-tier colleges, may need to reattempt |

**The Drawing section is critical** — 75 out of 200 marks. Students who can't draw well will struggle regardless of their MCQ score. This is fundamentally different from JEE — it tests artistic ability.

## How to Prepare

**When to start:** Drawing skills need time to develop — start at least 1 year before (Class 11).

**Strategy:**
- **Drawing (75 marks)** — Practice daily:
  - 3D object drawing (isometric, perspective)
  - Composition drawing (arranging given objects)
  - Architectural sketching (buildings, street scenes)
  - Memory drawing (draw a scene from memory)
- **Mathematics (Part A)** — Same as JEE level. PCM students are already prepared. Focus on algebra, trigonometry, coordinate geometry, and 3D geometry.
- **General Aptitude** — This includes visual perception, spatial reasoning, and architectural awareness. Practice:
  - 2D and 3D visualization questions
  - Texture, colour theory, and design principles
  - Basic physics of materials (stress, strain basics)

**Resources:**
- Drawing practice: Join a NATA coaching institute (B.Arch Guru, Mosaic, A-One) — online options available
- Previous year NATA papers (available on nata.in)
- "A Visual Dictionary of Architecture" by Francis Ching
- Practice 3D drawing from YouTube channels dedicated to NATA prep
